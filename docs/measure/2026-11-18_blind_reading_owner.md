### What decides the move

The move is rolled by the engine (data/rules/reply.yaml). Its weights are multiplied by the stance of the talk (busy, wary, tired, a
grievance), by trust in the player, and by two habits of the person's voice (how they put questions, how they refuse). **No trait enters
it.** So "cold" does not make the butcher likelier to give a word or two, and "talkative" does not make the herbalist less likely to.
A companion has no stance, so theirs is weighted by trust and those two habits alone.

### What it shows

- **A move in character did not win for being so.** Where the move plainly fitted the speaker, the owner chose the new reply 3 times and
  the reply as it is 6 times, and marked the new reply a machine's 4 times. Khash is told he "answers questions with as little as will
  do", and his word-or-two replies (pairs 5 and 49) still lost and were marked a machine's. The model readers went the same way on Khash
  (none of three for the new reply in 49 and 53) and liked the short replies of Jesna, who is warm (three of three in 22 and 28).
- **My reading of that: the fault is less that a cold move fell on a warm person, and more that the move stacks on a habit already
  there.** Khash is terse without it; told to give "a word or two" on top, he gives nothing to answer.
- **The move is not the only thing that differs between the two replies of a pair.** The game hands a companion a few of their habits
  with each reply, drawn afresh: in 8 of the 20 companion pairs the two replies were given different habits (pair 5: one reply was told
  "asks a question back" and asked one; the other was told "answers with as little as will do" and did). A townsperson's stance for the
  talk is drawn too: in 4 of the 32 townsfolk pairs it differed (pair 8: the talkative herbalist was "busy" for one reply and
  "nursing a grievance" for the other). Both are drawn by the engine, as the move is, so a pair compares more than the move.
- **Small numbers.** Nine choices of the owner's fall on a move marked in character, one on a move marked against.
