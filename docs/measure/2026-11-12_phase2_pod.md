## 6. What this means for f603 (the switch)

The owner set no bar in advance. Read against the plan's questions:

- **Judgement:** the act ruling is as good as the old one or better, with no false rejections in 67 judged cases.
- **Availability:** it is not ready. One improvised act in four found no ruling in play, and three answers in five failed in the
  corpus. Switching now would turn a quarter of improvised acts into "an attempt, waiting for its ruling".
- **Cost:** a turn on either path prices about the same; an act ruling is about twice as long to write as the old ruling.
- **Births and slots:** no change needed.

The failures are mechanical, not judgement, and each has an infrastructure fix (GOALS.md "Infrastructure over patches"). Built the
same day, after the owner's "fix them first":

1. **f606: the act ruling's grammar, made for the call.** Targets by code, each with only the change words that land on its kind,
   never a situation itself; the fail, the check and the capabilities from their lists; a no that must say what happens instead;
   who acts where a change is a person's own doing (a surrender, a flight, a warning), the state a change of state or of access
   gives; short free text (act.yaml `grammar`). This closes the loops (a fixed `fail` cannot repeat), the invented words, the
   targets named in words, the changes dropped for want of `who`, and the no without an answer. The answer's limit stays at 700
   tokens: the longest of the 177 complete answers was 277.
2. **f607: the corpus measures a fair ruling as fair.** A case may allow a list of fair changes ("terrified" or "surrenders") or
   forbid only the change that matters (a stance, not a feeling).

A first reading of the wrestle case said one person had two codes; the engine already maps a person's scene code to their situation
part, so nothing was needed there.

Then a short pod session (about an hour, under $1) runs the corpus on the new path again, and the switch is decided on those numbers.

## 8. The tuning measure (2026-11-13, f609 to f612)

One rented RTX 4090 (Secure Cloud, US-CA-2; the A6000 was out of stock), 05:14 to 06:01 UTC, about $0.70 at $0.89 an hour, the game at
f612 (f89d68a) sent with runpodctl's one-time transfer. The three case files on the act path, live, twice: once as the game ships, and
once with `DND_THINK_TASKS=act_ruling DND_THINK_ROOM=8192`.

| Corpus | Pass 1 | Pass 2 (thinking asked for) |
|---|---|---|
| First set (50) | 47 of 50: false acceptances the king (a plain yes) and the guild hall jump; false rejection the dark tunnel | 49 of 50: the guild hall jump |
| Complex (28) | 26 of 28: the dragon's hoard, the vault tunnel | 25 of 28: the dragon's hoard, the vault tunnel, the merchant signing his shop over |
| Acts (21) | 21 of 21 on sense; changes wrong 2: the granary, the wrestle | 21 of 21 on sense; changes wrong 3: the granary, the looters driven out, the wrestle |

**Every one of the 198 rulings was valid; none was cut off and none failed.**

**Thinking cannot help this call as the game makes it.** The request asked for it (`enable_thinking: true`, 8,892 tokens of room,
checked by hand on the pod), but the server sent back no thinking at all: the game's grammar holds from the first token, so the model
writes the JSON at once (134 tokens on the probe, the median of pass 2 130). Pass 2 is therefore a second sample of pass 1, and the
two passes show the corpus's noise at this size: one to three cases a file either way.

**What the act's ruling says it comes to (`works`, f609).** 143 written, none empty, the longest 217 characters (the limit is 300),
none with a dash (hyphenated words only). It bounds a claim well when the model's sense is right: the king "heard by the gathered
crowd", the merchant "weeps, but he does not sign over his shop", the hoard in pass 1 "faces a mix of greed and skepticism as you
shout your tale". When the sense is wrong it describes the impossible thing done: "reaches the roof of the guild hall", "digs a tunnel
under the guild hall, emerging inside the vault", the hoard in pass 2 "a large group of people begins to gather and follow the
character". The bound holds a success to the ruling; it cannot correct a ruling that accepts the impossible. The model often writes it
in the third person ("The Vanguard", "The character"), not the second the prompt asks for; the narrator retells it either way.

**Cost.** An act ruling now writes a median 188 tokens (pass 1; 130 in pass 2) against 158 in the rerun of 11-12, with a prompt about
65 tokens longer: about a second more on the 3060 (31.7 answer and 757 prompt tokens a second, data/measured/card_profile.yaml) in
pass 1, nothing in pass 2.

**The decided cases, as measured:**
- The king: pass 1 a plain yes with a bounded `works` (wrong under the owner's rule, a cost only); pass 2 a no. The dragon's hoard: a
  cost both times, but each asked for something to be made (a crate; the market crowd), so it counts wrong under the strict bound.
- The dark tunnel without darkvision: a no in pass 1 (the false rejection again), a yes in pass 2.
- The cart wheel: lifted by force on strength both times, right under f611.
- The granary: the danger lifted both times, the stallholder never made safe (no hint in the target line, as the owner chose).

**Two new findings, both in the engine's reach, not the model's judgement:**
1. *The wrestle, pass 1*: the model asked for the guard's surrender with no `because`, and the engine refused it ("they stand against
   the one who acted, and would not do it"), since only force moves one set against. The grammar does not require `because` on a
   change; requiring it would close this.
2. *The wrestle, pass 2*: the model asked for the guard to surrender, stay committed against the act, and grow warmer, all at once,
   and the engine kept all three. Contradictory changes on one person stand together.

**What this leaves for the switch's tuning:** the guild hall jump and the vault tunnel (accepted in both passes, with a `works` that
does the impossible thing) need the fallback the plan named, since thinking is no help here: a difficulty past "hard" that the engine
turns into a no. The wrestle's two findings are engine fixes. The rest is within the noise.
