# Three blind readers on decide then speak, beside the owner's reading

Read 18 November 2026. The owner asked for a blind reader to score the pairs too, and for three different ones.

## In short

- **None of the three reaches the bar** (the new speech preferred in at least 60%). Over the 52 pairs that test the switch the new speech
  took 50%, 51% and 38% of the choices made; on the owner's own 38 pairs, 53%, 58% and 39%. The owner's figure was 33%.
- **They agree with the owner about what loses**: a reply that keeps back or leaves out what was asked. "Keep back what they asked for"
  won none of 24 readings; "answer the part you care about" won 6 of 35 choices.
- **They agree that a question put in place of the answer reads as a machine's**: the "ask them something first" replies were marked so 27
  times in 48 readings.
- **They differ from the owner in two places**: the clerk lines, where they chose the reply that answers (16 of 18 readings) over
  the reply that says the clerk is away, and the short replies ("a word or two"), which they liked more than the owner did.
- **They are models, not people, and do not know the game's facts.** They chose as the owner did in 11 or 12 of the owner's 18 choices,
  which is a little better than a coin and far from the same reader.

## How it was done

- **The sheets.** Each reader had its own sheet of the 52 pairs that test the switch (the sixty, less the eight written by a call the
  switch does not touch): its own order, its own numbering, and the letters A and B swapped in half the pairs, a different half for each.
  Nothing on a sheet says which reply is which, and the 14 pairs with a reply marked against the game's records were left in, unmarked, so
  that what the readers make of them can be seen.
- **The readers.** Three fresh model sessions that knew nothing of this work and read only their own sheet. Reader 1 ran on this session's
  model and was asked to read as someone who reads stories for pleasure; Reader 2 ran on Opus, as someone who plays at a table; Reader 3
  ran on Sonnet, as an editor of dialogue.
- **The questions** were the owner's two: which reply is better, if either clearly is, and which reads as written by a machine (A, B,
  both or neither), with a line of why.
- **Scoring.** Each reader's letters were put back on the pairs' own numbers and set against the keys
  (2026-11-17_blind_pairs_key.md and 2026-11-17_blind_pairs_2_key.md). No line of any answer sheet failed to read.

## What stands out

- **The readers mostly agree with each other.** All three chose the same way in 32 of 52 pairs: the new reply in 13,
  the reply as it is in 16, no winner in 3.
- **The clerk lines carry the new speech's score.** Six pairs ask for the guild clerk, who has gone home (6, 11, 21, 32, 37, 44). In all six
  the reply as it is says the clerk is away, in a stiff set phrase, and the new reply answers as if the question were the speaker's own. The
  readers chose the new reply in 16 of 18 readings and marked the set phrase as a machine's. By the game's records the reply as it is is the
  accurate one, and in three of the six (11, 21, 44) the new reply says something unfounded or wrong. A reader who cannot see the records
  rewards a confident answer either way.
- **Without the "make light of it" pairs** (the six clerk pairs and pair 18) the three readers together chose the new reply 47 times and
  the reply as it is 75 times: 39% for the new, close to the owner's 33%. With them it is 66 to 76, 46%.
- **Companions lose more than townsfolk** under the new speech here (19 to 29, with 12 readings of no winner); townsfolk are level at 47
  each. The owner had it the other way round (townsfolk 2 to 8, companions 4 to 4). The clerk pairs are all a townsperson's: without them
  townsfolk stand at 31 to 46, much as companions do.
- **Reader 3, the editor, is the hardest on both arms**: 28 marks of a machine's hand on the new replies (the other two gave 13 and 14)
  and 21 on the replies as they are (16 and 14).

## The readers' reasons, in their own words

Model output, quoted as written. What they say they want is a reply that answers what was asked with something that can be seen or used:
a place, wolves and sheep, a small act. What they mark as a machine's: set phrases that read like a notice, a question tacked on the end,
stock weather and tide, and lines that answer nothing.

- **Pair 2.** All three chose the reply as it is, as the owner did.
  - Reader 1 (as it is): A has dialect and dry humour; to have any age is strange.
  - Reader 2 (as it is): A has a real voice; B is a muddled tautology.
  - Reader 3 (as it is): B is voicey and logical; A illogical.
- **Pair 48.** All three chose the new reply, against the owner.
  - Reader 1 (new): A names wolves and sheep; B piles vague metaphors and says little.
  - Reader 2 (new): A gives wolves and sheep; B drifts into stock storm imagery.
  - Reader 3 (new): B concrete with wolves; A is stock "tide turning" metaphor.
- **Pair 21.** A clerk pair: all three chose the new reply, which the game's records do not bear out.
  - Reader 1 (new): A answers the question; B is a stiff refusal.
  - Reader 2 (new): B answers; A is a formula saying nothing.
  - Reader 3 (new): B answers; A says nothing useful.
- **Pair 9.** "Ask them something first": split, the owner chose the reply as it is.
  - Reader 1 (as it is): B tells me what she does sell; A just deflects.
  - Reader 2 (new): A asks me something back.
  - Reader 3 (as it is): B answers; A tacks on a stock question.
- **Pair 5.** "A word or two": two chose the new reply, the owner the reply as it is.
  - Reader 1 (new): B is terse and human; A sounds like a counsellor.
  - Reader 2 (as it is): B engages with what I said.
  - Reader 3 (new): B short and human; A therapist-like probing question.

## What this is worth

- It is a second opinion, not a second owner. The bar is the owner's preference, and that reading is done (33%, not met).
- Three models of one family share tastes; their agreement with each other is not three independent votes.
- They read each pair cold, without the scene, the person or the game's records, which is how a reader meets a line but not how a
  player meets it.
- What they add is where they and the owner agree from different starting points: the withholding moves lose, and a question that
  takes the answer's place reads as a machine's.
