# The sixty blind pairs against the game's records

Asked for by the owner on 17 November: which reply in each pair is accurate. Every reply was set beside what the game recorded for that
line: who was there, what the speaker was given, what was bought or struck, and what the speaker's own past is. Marks are by letter. Which
letter is the new speech stays in the keys.

- **Accurate**: what it says matches the game.
- **Part unfounded**: the answer is right, with one thing added that the game did not give.
- **Unfounded**: it states as fact something the game did not give (it may happen to be true).
- **Wrong**: it goes against the game's record.
- **Nothing to check**: a reaction or a question; no fact of the game in it.

**Eight pairs are not a test of the switch** and should not count toward the bar: 13, 19, 27, 29, 31, 38, 39 and 59. In seven the replies
are companions talking among themselves, written by a call the switch does not touch; in pair 13 one reply is the game's own question. That
was a fault in how the pairs were gathered. Fifty-two pairs remain: 18 of the first twenty, 34 of the second forty.

## I ask the clerk which of these jobs is closest.

The clerk (Aldley Rushton) has gone home at that hour; the one answering is the butcher, who keeps a counter in the guild house. She was given the seven jobs on the board, and two distances: the south fields are a few minutes' walk and the Greywood a short walk. She was not given where yarrow or silverleaf grow, nor how far the other jobs are. In the world yarrow grows mostly in the south fields and silverleaf mostly in the Greywood; the nearest job of all is the delivery to the temple, inside the town.

## What the game itself is missing here, whichever speech is kept

1. **Someone asked for who is away.** The game knows the clerk works there and has gone home. The line goes to whoever is present, and saying so is left to the model.
2. **"Which is closest" has an exact answer the speaker is not given.** The board is handed over without where each job is or how far.
3. **A line about a thing the game does not hold** (the knife; the plant that was never picked) reaches the speaker as if the thing were there. Some replies then describe it.
4. **People present are named without saying he or she**, so a speaker guesses (Hys Oakwell, both replies of pair 58).
5. **After a fight with no one hurt, "whoever's hurt worst" is still put to a companion**, who is told only that the fight was won easily. One reply made up a wound.

None of these is built. Each would be a feature of its own.
