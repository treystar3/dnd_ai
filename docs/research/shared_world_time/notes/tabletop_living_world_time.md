# Tabletop and text role-play practice for shared campaign time across many groups

Provenance note for the report writer. Quotes below were retrieved through a page-summarising fetch tool, so wording is as relayed by that tool; quotes marked "(search snippet)" came from a search result summary and the page itself was not opened (or refused the fetch). Where a URL could not be tied to a snippet with confidence, that is said. The web search budget for the session ran out before every gap could be closed; remaining gaps are listed per section. "Fit" in the Inferences always means fit for: a host-authoritative co-op CRPG narrated in text, where one typed action can consume seconds or days, players split and reunite in one persistent world, the engine owns every number (healing, rent, quest timers), and there is no player cap.

## 1. "1:1 time", "time jail", and locking a character versus moving a lagging character forward

### Takeaway
The BrOSR rule is: between sessions the world clock runs at one game day per real day, a session may run a party any distance into the future, and a character whose own date is ahead of the world date cannot be played until the real calendar reaches it ("time jail"); the model only works because each player keeps a stable of characters and because sessions rarely collide in both time and place. The opposite rule, found in one actively documented per-character-clock campaign and in several single-calendar West Marches servers, lets or forces lagging characters to spend the gap as downtime up to (never past) the most advanced character's date, forbids back-dated orders, and treats the gap as a resource ("slack") rather than a lock.

### Cited Findings

**The rule itself (lock the character who is ahead)**
- Definition used by practitioners: "For each day in the real world, one day passes in the campaign world"; in-session time advances as the adventure requires (example: a week's travel, a day of delving and the return, 3 to 4 hours of play, 15 campaign days). A party that ends a session in the campaign future stays there: "Until real time catches up to them they're still on their adventure, and you can't play those characters." — [Tales from the Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/)
- A second practitioner's formulation: "When a session is finished, players 'snap back' to the time the session started", the finished session's events stay on the calendar as fixed history, and time jail is "a state where a PC is, by the calendar, still finishing an adventure or task." Players in time jail "need to play another character" chosen "from among this stable of PCs". — [Primeval Patterns, Timekeeping in TTRPGs Part 2](https://primevalpatterns.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-ttrpgs-part-2)
- A commenter's plain statement of the lock on Jeffro's blog: if the party passes a week in game, those characters cannot act for a week of real time; if they pass 50 days, they are unavailable for 50 days. Suggested relief: patron players declare actions resolved over several sessions, or players use other characters on a parallel timeline. — [Jeffro's Space Gaming Blog, "When No Play Is Happening" (comments)](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2022/04/14/when-no-play-is-happening/)
- Jeffro on why he never bends the rule: the group has sometimes wanted to "suspend or alter or fudge" strict time but "it never makes it into the game", because explaining a deviation to many independent players and getting it to "stick" is "all but impossible". — [Jeffro, "When No Play Is Happening"](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2022/04/14/when-no-play-is-happening/)
- The source text the movement cites is AD&D DMG pp. 37-38: "it is best to use 1 actual day = 1 game day when no play is happening". — [EN World, "Time in the Campaign" (quoting the DMG)](https://www.enworld.org/threads/time-in-the-campaign.690348/post-8715428)
- Whether it is a rule at all is disputed: "1:1 time IS NOT RAW" (Theory of Games); "1:1 time is a misleading term because the overall flow of time in a campaign is usually not going to be 1:1" (Lunamancer). — [theRPGsite, "How can 1:1 time work during a delve?"](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/how-can-11-time-work-during-a-delve/15)

**Who advances time; what happens when groups are apart**
- The world date is the real calendar; nobody advances it. Each party's own date is advanced by its session. Shared calendar position is what decides "who arrives first" at a contested place; the Border Baronies author rejects per-character timelines because with "too many disparate elements moving at the same time" they become hard to manage. — [Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/)
- Referee benefit claimed: real-time pacing gives preparation time ("If something's going to happen in a week, you have a week to figure out what's going to happen"), and in a weekly game monster lairs "will generally have a week to prepare for the players' return". — [Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/); [Jeffro, "How do you do Patron style play in D&D?"](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2021/07/17/how-do-you-do-patron-style-play-in-dd/)
- Downtime between sessions is real elapsed days: characters can gather information, research spells, recruit hirelings, spy, attempt assassinations, and "can die out of session". — [Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/)

**Multiple characters per player; patron and tier play**
- Multiple characters are the stated cure for the lock: "That's how you sort out the Time Jail", players should keep about three characters (King Tyranno). — [theRPGsite delve thread](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/how-can-11-time-work-during-a-delve/15)
- A played example: after a ten-day trek, "all six players had to roll up new PCs because Yllmeeton and his companions still had 3 days left to travel"; "all of us wound up running two (or even three!) PCs which were based in different locations". — [Stephen Smith, "Timekeeping in D&D RPGs"](https://stephensmith.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-d-and-d-rpgs)
- In Jeffro's Trollopulous campaign time jail decides what gets played: mid-level parties that travel far "must remain in time jail", and 1:1 time and time jail are "a major determinant of whether we play adventurer tier or conqueror tier in a given session". Backup characters (for example an assassin "as a backup when their main character is training") run spy missions in downtime. — [Jeffro, "Trollopulous and the Multi-Tier Campaign"](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2023/03/13/trollopulous-and-the-multi-tier-campaign/)
- Patron play: "Turn over a major patron or faction of the town over to each player"; it forms an "'always on' campaign that runs in the background of your players' adventures". Jeffro admits factions "can be really hard to adjudicate fairly and consistently", and that patron and name-level play needs "intense referee attention that coincides with the dedicated attention of many elite-level players at once", which he now confines to planned seasonal events that end with those players returned to a quasi-static state. — [Jeffro, patron post](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2021/07/17/how-do-you-do-patron-style-play-in-dd/); [Jeffro, multi-tier post](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2023/03/13/trollopulous-and-the-multi-tier-campaign/)

**Contradictions between groups**
- Practitioners admit there is no complete answer: on preventing paradox "we don't really know"; mitigations are small sessions and limits on how far forward one session may jump; "Events from two sessions must collide in time and in space in order to create a paradox"; when an area already has a recorded future "it is sometimes necessary to adventure elsewhere to avoid creating a paradox." — [Primeval Patterns Part 2](https://primevalpatterns.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-ttrpgs-part-2)
- A megadungeon referee using real-time-between-sessions requires that "every session begins and ends outside the dungeon"; when he allowed safe-place stays inside, it caused "some headaches around where characters exist along assorted timelines and how these timelines interact"; fixed-date events (a festival, a time-limited site) collided with a player's long mission and "I haven't figured out a solution to this dilema yet". — [Underworld Adventurer, "An Unexpected Turn"](https://underworldadventurer.substack.com/p/an-unexpected-turn)

**Mid-dungeon session ends**
- Advice is to plan the return before the session ends; one table lets a party still in the future have another session before the datum catches up and "resume where they left off", which does not disturb the timeline. — [Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/)
- Critic's view from play: "I still cannot see how 1:1 Timekeeping works with a classic Megadungeon delve." — [Stephen Smith](https://stephensmith.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-d-and-d-rpgs)

**What critics say**
- Locked out: players "were locked out of going on any other adventures while we waited for the current one to resolve" (KrisSnow, quoted); it "would be annoying from a player perspective if you can only have a single character" (shoplifter). — [theRPGsite, "Don't fall for the strict timekeeping fad"](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/don-t-fall-for-the-strict-timekeeping-fad/45)
- Long projects: a castle build pushes the next session "3 years" out (DocJones). — [theRPGsite delve thread](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/how-can-11-time-work-during-a-delve/15)
- Lifespan: "If you always play 1-to-1, most YOU AND YOUR PLAYERS will be dead before you ever get to play a venerable dwarf" (Eric Diaz, quoted). — [theRPGsite strict timekeeping thread](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/don-t-fall-for-the-strict-timekeeping-fad/45)
- Fairness: "the people with the most real world time to play games have the most advanced characters"; "logistically challenging" (Malmuria). — [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/time-in-the-campaign.690348/post-8715428)
- From a player who tried it: "1:1 Timekeeping put a rather extreme limitation on us that became a sort of damper on the joy of the game play." — [Stephen Smith](https://stephensmith.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-d-and-d-rpgs)
- From an AD&D traditionalist: "Using 1:1 time in ALL matters, makes the careful tracking of time UN-necessary"; he reads "training jail" as game weeks to be waited out in elastic time, not real weeks, while granting 1:1 is "easier" than tracking each character by day. — [B/X Blackrazor, "Jeffro's D&D"](http://bxblackrazor.blogspot.com/2022/07/jeffros-d.html)
- Scale: the Border Baronies author first calls it "obviously only usable in big groups" (later arguing it helps 4 to 8 players too); shoplifter calls real time "the only fair way" for a rotating cast of 20 or more. — [Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/); [theRPGsite](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/don-t-fall-for-the-strict-timekeeping-fad/45)

**The alternative: move the lagging character forward (per-character clocks with a frontier)**
- One running D&D campaign ("A Fantastic Medieval Wargames Campaign") gives every character its own date. Between sessions each clock ticks 1:1 with real days; in a session a clock may jump. A published "days behind" column shows how far each character trails "the 'globally' most advanced characters". Downtime orders may run faster than 1:1 and advance a character's clock "until hitting the clock of the most advanced character": "So they can't advance the global game clock, but they can catch the character up to it." Example: a character at January 30 travels to join a character dated February 12 so both can play one session. — [Erin, Campaign News December 17 (2025)](https://erin1478.blogspot.com/2025/12/campaign-news-december-17.html)
- Same campaign, the constraints: no retroactive use of the gap ("If a character is already at such and so date, it is not possible to issue back-dated orders"; "It's an opportunity cost that can only be paid in advance to be meaningful"); the referee can hold a clock while he processes orders ("I held the characters' clocks to the date at which those characters would have had the information") and "character time begins ticking again" on delivery; and unused slack can be lost ("It's possible that I will need to force clock synchronizations at some point"). The referee calls per-character dates a heavy cognitive load that may eventually cause a serious error. — [Erin, Campaign News December 17](https://erin1478.blogspot.com/2025/12/campaign-news-december-17.html)
- Softer variants from forum referees: "bank" the real time elapsed when advancing the calendar would not make sense (for example a session that stopped just before an encounter) and apply it later (Musing Mage); "I will pause fictional time however if letting time pass is unreasonable", and letting players fast-forward "within reason" so "a single session could span 2 months of time" when one character was bedridden for a month (Old Aegidius); alternate weeks for two groups so game days never overlap (S'mon). — [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/time-in-the-campaign.690348/post-8715428); [theRPGsite delve thread](https://therpgsite.com/pen-paper-roleplaying-games-rpgs-discussion/how-can-11-time-work-during-a-delve/15)
- The Alexandrian's open table accepts inconsistency instead: "every individual character will have their own personal continuity that will make sense", though there "won't really be a consistent 'multiverse' if you look at things too closely"; absent characters "were busy doing downtime activities" and the GM asks "What have you been up to since last we met?". Session-end rule: "you have to get out of the dungeon before you can go home for the night", ending play "15-30 minutes before your actual end time", with his Escaping the Dungeon tables for parties that do not make it. — [The Alexandrian, "Opening Your Game Table" (post and comments)](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1223/roleplaying-games/opening-your-game-table); [Escaping the Dungeon](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/2149/roleplaying-games/escaping-the-dungeon)

### Inferences
- The two rules are mirror images around one invariant: characters may interact only when their dates are equal. Time jail satisfies it by freezing the character who is ahead until the slowest clock (the real calendar) arrives. The advance rule satisfies it by pushing the character who is behind up to the frontier, charging the gap as downtime. Lock costs the ahead player play time; advance costs the behind player unplanned days (and whatever the engine bills per day).
- Time jail depends on things a computer game does not have to accept: a wall-clock world date, sessions as the unit of play, and a stable of spare characters. Its one transferable idea is that a character who commits to a long action becomes unavailable as an actor until the frontier reaches the action's end, and that this is tolerable only if the player has something else to do.
- The per-character-clock campaign is the closest tabletop analogue to the stated CRPG. Its rules translate almost directly into engine rules: (a) each character carries a date; (b) world frontier = the maximum character date; (c) a lagging character has slack = frontier minus own date, spendable only forward on engine-priced downtime; (d) no back-dated orders, because cost must be paid before the outcome is known; (e) meeting requires equal dates, so the earlier character advances first; (f) a forced synchronisation exists as an escape hatch. The referee's stated pain (cognitive load, risk of error) is exactly what an engine removes.
- The unsolved tabletop problem is the one an engine must solve explicitly: a lagging character entering a place whose later state is already recorded. Tabletop answers are "adventure elsewhere", "Destiny decreed the monster was elsewhere" style patching, or forced sync. The Primeval Patterns observation that a paradox needs collision in both time and space suggests scoping the equal-date requirement to shared places and shared objects rather than to the whole world: a place could carry its own latest-touched date, and entering it could advance the entrant to that date.
- Critics' strongest practical complaint is about being unable to play, not about bookkeeping. Any lock in a live co-op game should therefore be on the character's ability to affect shared state, not on the player's ability to do something.
- The "world moves in real time while nobody plays" half of 1:1 time is separable and is a poor fit for an engine that owns rent and timers: rent accruing while the game is closed would bill players for not playing, which is the fairness complaint Malmuria raises.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary Jeffro post that states the time-jail rule in rule-like words; the clearest statements are from followers (Border Baronies, Primeval Patterns) and a commenter on his blog. His post "When No Play Is Happening" cites DMG pp. 37-38 without quoting it.
- The DMG's own treatment of crossing timelines (the worked example with parties A to F and its advice on calling a halt when one group has run far enough ahead) was not re-fetched because the requester already has it; a commenter says the DMG "covers parallel timelines" and "what happens when the timelines cross" ([Jeffro comments](https://jeffro.wordpress.com/2022/04/14/when-no-play-is-happening/)). My recollection is that the DMG passage itself contains both a "call a halt" instruction for the group that is ahead and an acceptance of diverging timelines; that recollection was not verified in this session.
- No source found that measures how often time jail actually idles players, or any retrospective from a 1:1 campaign that ended because of it. RPGPundit's claim that "the BroSR has never managed to make a single long-term campaign work" is contested in the same thread and is unsupported there.
- Whether the Erin campaign has since had to force a synchronisation, and how, is not known.

## 2. West Marches and living-world Discord servers: written time rules

### Takeaway
Real rule documents fall into three families: a single shared campaign date that every expedition advances (absent characters are assumed to have waited in town); real time as the clock, with in-game durations restated in real days; and no calendar at all, with "downtime days/points" issued per session or per real day as a currency. Nearly all of them avoid the mid-dungeon problem by rule ("every game starts and ends in town"), and the explicit anti-paradox rules are short: one event per calendar date per character, no joining an event earlier than your character's latest date, and item quarantine between overlapping games.

### Cited Findings

**Single shared date, sequential expeditions (The Ashen Marches)**
- "The campaign uses one shared in-game date." "If an expedition lasts 6 days, the campaign calendar advances 6 days." "Each adventure begins on the current campaign date." "The next adventure begins after the previous adventure ends." — [The Ashen Marches wiki, Time Tracking](https://www.westmarches.games/communities/the-ashen-marches/wiki/time-tracking-in-the-ashen-marches)
- Absent characters are moved forward implicitly: "There are no character-specific calendars or return dates." "Characters who did not participate are assumed to have remained in Last Lantern during that time." — [Ashen Marches wiki](https://www.westmarches.games/communities/the-ashen-marches/wiki/time-tracking-in-the-ashen-marches)
- Ordering disputes are settled by staff: when sessions are ambiguous "the GMs determine which adventure happened first." World events are calendar-driven: "If the campaign calendar reaches the date of a scheduled event, that event occurs." "The world does not wait forever." — [Ashen Marches wiki](https://www.westmarches.games/communities/the-ashen-marches/wiki/time-tracking-in-the-ashen-marches)
- Downtime there accrues from days spent in town: a character may take one downtime action when it has accrued 7 downtime days in Last Lantern, each day in town counts, and players track their own days on the official calendar or their sheet (search snippet describing the same wiki). — [Ashen Marches wiki](https://www.westmarches.games/communities/the-ashen-marches/wiki/time-tracking-in-the-ashen-marches)

**Shared calendar table with per-character busy marks (a multi-DM Roll20 shared campaign)**
- Anti-paradox rules as written by the organiser: "A PC cannot participate in more than one event on any given calendar date." "A PC cannot participate in an event that takes him/her back in time." — [Roll20 forum thread](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/4229851)
- Downtime is the complement of adventuring on the shared calendar: PCs "earn downtime when they are not involved in a game"; "Each day is a one point of downtime"; "A PC never earns downtime until after its first session"; on the log "you put an X for each PC that was busy adventuring during that time", and "The rest of us DMs will put the downtime in when we get to that time period." — [Roll20 forum thread](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/4229851)
- Recommended practice: "Having a separate PC for each ongoing adventure you are involved in." — [Roll20 forum thread](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/4229851)
- Complaints from participants: "There have been multiple cases of the same date appearing more than once on the table"; "Many of the entries on time spans are also incorrect"; a new GM: "The problem is as a new GM I have no clue as to the downtime to put in"; one player found tracking two characters' downtime confusing and asked GMs to post session start dates. — [Roll20 forum thread](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/4229851)

**Real time as the clock, no in-game calendar (Colville West Marches, Avrae-based)**
- No calendar is defined; long durations are restated in real days: "An extended rest is 30 days in real life"; a daily-for-a-year spell "only needs to be cast every day for 30 IRL days"; clone maturity becomes "30 IRL days"; a 7-day spell effect lasts "the next 2 quests they share". — [CWM Player's Guide](https://gmbinder.com/share/-LAmPCMCp-4L2HdO0mUT)
- A character in an overlapping play-by-post game and voice sessions is ordered by rule: "Any voice session is assumed to run some time after the PBP session concluded." "If you die in the PBP, then any voice session is assumed to have run some time before the PBP." Items are quarantined: "Items and equipment gained in Voice sessions played while the PBP is running cannot be used within the PBP", and the reverse until the PBP concludes; consumables used "must be removed from both character sheets". — [CWM Player's Guide](https://gmbinder.com/share/-LAmPCMCp-4L2HdO0mUT)
- Tools: "Most games are run on Discord using Avrae"; a "Dragonspeaker" role sets up "server aliases and functions like downtime tracking". Downtime "allows characters to pursue long-term activities between game sessions" (details in a separate document not retrieved). Catch-up is social, not mechanical: "DMs are asked to prioritize players that have played less frequently." — [CWM Player's Guide](https://gmbinder.com/share/-LAmPCMCp-4L2HdO0mUT)

**Downtime as a per-session currency, sessions closed at town (Taverna Aventurilor West Marches, Eberron)**
- "Every game starts in Quickstone and ends in Quickstone." "The trip back is usually handled off-screen unless fictionally unsafe." "Loot, discoveries, and advancement are finalized at session end when you're back in town." "Unrecovered loot remains where it fell (unless someone else finds it)." — [WM Player's & DM's Guide v1.0.4](https://www.legendkeeper.com/p/cmgfh3rud2hje0jlh7dj6eqvn/g8k1o0c0)
- One Downtime Point per expedition; "1 Downtime Point counts as 3 days of downtime"; "DP must be used after each expedition" (no banking, except points earned by DMing); "Crafting progress and spent resources carry over between sessions." — [WM Player's & DM's Guide v1.0.4](https://www.legendkeeper.com/p/cmgfh3rud2hje0jlh7dj6eqvn/g8k1o0c0)
- The only timestamp is the real date embedded in a hex exploration code (`NW.251016.Y`, with `.1`, `.2` suffixes for two sessions the same day); "Multiple Characters: Allowed (helps parties form quickly)." — [WM Player's & DM's Guide v1.0.4](https://www.legendkeeper.com/p/cmgfh3rud2hje0jlh7dj6eqvn/g8k1o0c0)

**Other documents seen at snippet level**
- An Earthdawn living server ran "1 real life week = 1 month in Barsaive", with downtime on a "2 days=1 week" basis (search snippet). — [FASA Games forum](https://fasagames.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=3745)
- A World Anvil hosted West Marches campaign grants one week of downtime after each session and defers to Xanathar's Guide for activities (search snippet; the URL attribution is my best match and is not confirmed). — [World Anvil, "Moot: West Marches"](https://www.worldanvil.com/w/dromaria/a/moot3A-west-marches-article)
- Sidhranor (Old School Essentials, Discord with Avrae): open scheduling ("A player can play when they want, as many times as they want"), calendar and downtime on separate pages not retrieved. — [Sidhranor player's guide](https://sites.google.com/view/dreaming-isle-ttrpg/players-guide/how-to-play)
- An OSE house-rule set makes "each real-world week ... one in-game week, a downtime turn", one downtime action per turn, with extra weeks passing by majority vote of active players (search snippet; I could not confirm which of the result URLs carries it).

### Inferences
- The single-shared-date model (Ashen Marches) is "advance the laggers" in its bluntest form: every expedition drags everyone's date forward and non-participants are deemed to have idled in town. It prevents all date mismatches by forbidding overlap, which works only because sessions are sequential. With simultaneous split parties in one live world it would mean one party's three-day action instantly ages everyone else, which is the behaviour the CRPG must decide whether to accept.
- The Roll20 group's two rules ("one event per calendar date", "never back in time") are the minimal machine-checkable consistency constraints for per-character timelines on a shared calendar, and its complaints (duplicate dates, wrong spans, GMs unsure what to award) are all human bookkeeping failures an engine would not have.
- Downtime-as-currency servers have no shared clock to keep consistent; they pay for that by having no persistent time-driven world (no rent, no timers). They are a precedent for pricing activities in days per character, not for a world calendar.
- The CWM overlap rule is a compact causality patch: order the overlapping threads after the fact so that death is always last, and quarantine gains across the overlap. That is a retroactive ordering rule plus an inventory firewall, usable where two threads of the same character cannot be serialised at the time of play. A host-authoritative engine never needs it because one character cannot be in two live threads.
- "Start and end in town" is universal because humans cannot leave a party suspended mid-dungeon across sessions with a moving calendar. An engine can suspend a party in place; the rule's remaining value is as a design hint that a safe place is the natural point at which to settle slack, bill upkeep and synchronise.

### Gaps
- The largest 5e living-world servers keep their rules in Discord channels or private documents; I found no indexed rule text for a server with a formal real-time-to-game-time ratio plus a bot-enforced downtime ledger, and no documentation of a dedicated calendar or downtime bot beyond Avrae aliases.
- The Ashen Marches "Downtime In Last Lantern" page and the CWM Downtime Rules document were not retrieved.
- No server document was found that handles a party left mid-dungeon at session end other than by forbidding it or handwaving the return.
- r/westmarches and r/osr threads were not reachable through the tools used, so player complaints here come from one Roll20 thread only.

## 3. Organised play: Living Greyhawk time units, Adventurers League downtime days, Pathfinder Society downtime

### Takeaway
Living Greyhawk is the direct precedent for time as a per-character budget: 52 time units per real calendar year, each adventure and many off-screen activities debited from it, upkeep charged per unit, and a character with none left unplayable until 1 January; it throttled power well enough but produced lockout, heavy bookkeeping and distorting sinks, and the campaign stopped charging adventures time in its final year. Its successors kept "days" only as a spendable reward (Adventurers League) or a use-it-now allowance (Pathfinder Society), with no calendar and no cap.

### Cited Findings

**Living Greyhawk time units (RPGA, 2000 to 2008)**
- Budget and lock: "At the start of each calendar year, each character in the campaign started with 52 time units (TUs)." "Once a character spent 52 TUs, that character could no longer be played until the start of the following calendar year." Workaround: "Once one character had run out of TUs, the player could simply switch to playing another character." — [Wikipedia, Living Greyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Greyhawk)
- Costs: "Most regional adventures cost 1 TU to play"; "most Core adventures cost 2 TUs"; visitors from another region could play regional adventures "at double the cost of Time Units". — [Wikipedia, Living Greyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Greyhawk)
- Purpose: 1 TU was "ostensibly equal to one week of game time" but was "an arbitrary number designed to limit how many adventures one character could play in a calendar year", restricting "how much wealth and power a single character could accumulate in a single year." — [Wikipedia, Living Greyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Greyhawk)
- A regional FAQ's wording: "Time Units (TUs) represent the how much your PC can do in a year." "At the beginning of each calendar year, your PC receives 52 TUs to spend on adventuring", or on "joining organizations or creating magical items (among other things)". "When that PC is out of TUs, you cannot play him again until next year." "The campaign year runs from January 1st to December 31st." The campaign "does not limit players to just one PC." — [Archclericy of Veluna FAQ](https://www.veluna.gilkison.net/whatis/faq.html)
- Upkeep was billed per time unit: "Its 12 gp per TU of a module for advnaturers standard which refreshes rations and spell components and arrows"; "Not much reason to go rich or Luxury except for roleplay reasons" (heirodule, 2003). — [EN World, "[Living Greyhawk] First time player"](https://www.enworld.org/threads/living-greyhawk-first-time-player-needs-help.47470/)
- Sinks beyond adventures: "Item creation is a big TU sink in LG"; "If you play alot, the TUs burn up"; "metaorg TU costs are steep now"; "Meta-orgs cost TU (time units) to join, and sometimes you advanced levels are only accessible after a certain time and spending even more TU"; modules sometimes offered "a choice to spend extra TU to perform some task"; and "it's easy to lose time units by breaking some law and ending up in jail." — [EN World thread](https://www.enworld.org/threads/living-greyhawk-first-time-player-needs-help.47470/)
- Adapted hardcover adventures cost a large share of the year: Expedition to the Demonweb Pits 22 TU, Expedition to the Ruins of Greyhawk 32 TU, Red Hand of Doom 51 TU; players who hit zero mostly did so through these; one player: "even though we played like crazy, I never hit zero Time Units with a single character"; another "appreciated the time units to attempt to curb power players" yet was "equally frustrated by this" (search snippet; the thread itself refused the fetch, so treat figures as unverified). — [RPG.net, "I miss Living Greyhawk"](https://forum.rpg.net/index.php?threads/i-miss-living-greyhawk.807810/)
- Bookkeeping complaint: "bookkeeping in Living Greyhawk got to be a bit onerous", with players "keeping track of TUs (time units), favors, and any curses" and walking around "with two inch binders"; flat upkeep did not scale: "At 15th level, you were still paying the same 12 gold for upkeep that you were at level 1." Living Forgotten Realms by contrast: "each character's career can be tracked on a few spreadsheet-style sheets of paper", it "really loosened up the onerous rules that were aimed at simulation", at the cost of "the lack of regional flavor". — [Roving Band of Misfits, "Living Greyhawk vs. Living Forgotten Realms"](https://rovingbandofmisfits.wordpress.com/2010/10/20/living-greyhawk-vs-living-forgotten-realms-wheres-the-debate/)
- The end state: after the campaign's end was announced "the rules concerning TUs were relaxed"; from 1 November 2007 adventures no longer cost TUs, though in-game activities such as crafting magic items still did, to "allow each character an unrestricted amount of play to reach 16th level." — [Wikipedia, Living Greyhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Greyhawk)
- Each adventure record tracked gold, experience and time units spent (search snippet). — [Greyhawk Online wiki, Living Greyhawk](https://greyhawkonline.com/greyhawkwiki/LG)

**Adventurers League downtime days (D&D 5e)**
- Days are a ledger entry, not a date: "Deduct the days from your total on your adventure logsheet and note the activity and days spent in the adventure notes." — [Berkeley Adventurers League, Downtime (summarising the Player's Guide)](https://sites.google.com/site/berkeleyadventuresleague/shared-rules/5th-edition-rules/downtime)
- Priced activities: spellcasting services cost one downtime day; trading a permanent magic item costs each party 15 downtime days unless they are at the same table; recuperating 3 days; carousing 5 days; training a language or tool 250 days at 1 gp per day; crafting 5 gp of value per day, and "Other characters can contribute their own downtime days to help"; copying spells per 8-hour day in the DM's presence; "Catching Up" available at 4th, 10th and 16th level to advance to the next level; leaving Ravenloft cost 20 downtime days. "You don't pay lifestyle when spending downtime." — [Berkeley AL, Downtime](https://sites.google.com/site/berkeleyadventuresleague/shared-rules/5th-edition-rules/downtime)
- Earning rule changed with the reward system: in Season 9 a character earned 10 downtime days on gaining a level (20 at tiers 2 to 4) (search snippet). — [Berkeley AL, Season 9 Rewards](https://sites.google.com/site/berkeleyadventuresleague/character-creation/season-9-rewards)
- By Season 11: "you now gain 10 downtime days after every adventure, not when you level up" (search snippet). — [The Legitimate Business, "11 things to know about AL D&D Season 11"](https://thelegitimatebusiness.com/2021/11-things-to-know-about-al-dnd-season-11/)

**Pathfinder Society (second edition) Downtime**
- Design intent at launch: "At the end of each adventure, a PC earns Downtime measured in days (3 days per 1 XP)"; "you can't stockpile Downtime", "it's up to you to use it before the next adventure"; the first-edition equivalent was a single Day Job check; the aim was for "the Pathfinder Society experience to match how everyone else is playing the game." — [Paizo blog, "Pathfinder Society Second Edition Preview: Earned Benefits"](https://paizo.com/community/blog/v5748dyo6sglh)
- Guide text quoted by players: "Quests grant 2 days of Downtime." "You can attempt one Earn Income check per 8-day cycle, including when you perform multiple activities during that cycle"; "A check to Earn Income does not carry beyond the 8-day cycle for which you attempt the check." — [Paizo forum, "A somewhat silly circumstance regarding Downtime"](https://paizo.com/threads/rzs42q6m)
- Later values as summarised in forum posts: quest 2 days, scenario 8 days, sanctioned adventure or Adventure Path typically 24 days; field-commissioned characters earn 50% more (3, 12, 36) (search snippet). — [Paizo blog, "Organized Play Update: Increased Rewards for Sanctioned Content"](https://paizo.com/blog/organized-play-update-increased-rewards-for-sanctioned-content)
- Complaint: the "8 day unit" wording seemed to block Earn Income for a 2-day quest grant and was hard to explain to new players; staff said a block shorter than 8 days is usable and the wording would be revised. Other threads question whether it is worth the trouble at all. — [Paizo forum](https://paizo.com/threads/rzs42q6m); [Paizo forum, "Downtime and Earn Income worth the trouble?"](https://paizo.com/threads/rzs43ppw)

### Inferences
- Living Greyhawk separates two things the CRPG could also separate: the fiction's date (the campaign year simply tracked the real year) and each character's spendable time. Adventures did not have dates relative to each other; they had costs. Order of play was order of record. This is why thousands of tables never contradicted each other on time: the only shared temporal fact was the year.
- What the budget solved: (1) power throttling across unequal real-life play time; (2) a common unit to price non-adventure activity (crafting, organisation membership, travel out of region, jail) against adventuring; (3) upkeep with a natural billing period. What it broke: hard lockout at zero (softened only by extra characters), long adventures eating most of a year, a flat per-unit upkeep that became trivial, and paper overhead ("Living Accounting" is the nickname the search result attributes to the campaign, source not opened).
- The 2007 change is the instructive part: when the designers wanted unrestricted play, they kept time costs for elective activities and removed them from adventuring. Time as a price for elective shortcuts survived; time as a cap on play did not.
- For the CRPG, the reusable pieces are the per-character time ledger with engine-priced debits and per-unit upkeep, not the annual cap. A cap exists to make strangers' characters comparable across a public campaign; a co-op game with one host has no such need. The lockout complaint also predicts how players would receive any rule that says "you may not act until the calendar turns".
- Adventurers League and Pathfinder Society show the fully decoupled end point: "days" that are only a token. Useful as evidence that players accept time-priced services (15 days to trade an item, 3 days to recuperate) as plain arithmetic, and that an allowance which cannot be banked (Pathfinder Society) produces edge-case confusion that a bankable ledger (Adventurers League) does not.
- "Other characters can contribute their own downtime days to help" (AL crafting) is a small precedent for pooling per-character time on one task.

### Gaps
- The Living Greyhawk Campaign Sourcebook text itself was not retrieved (the Scribd copy of v7.0.1 would not render), so rich and luxury upkeep rates, exact crafting TU formulas, and the sourcebook's own statement of purpose are missing. The 12 gp standard upkeep figure rests on two secondary sources.
- The adapted-module TU costs (22, 32, 51) come from a search snippet of a thread that refused the fetch.
- No RPGA statement was found on why Living Forgotten Realms did not carry time units forward; the reasons above are a player's reading.
- Adventurers League earning rates for Seasons 1 to 8 (per hours played) and the Season 10 intermediate rule were not confirmed from a primary guide; the downloaded v8.2 Player's Guide PDF could not be text-extracted in this environment.
- The current Pathfinder Society guide text was not retrieved directly.

## 4. Virtual tabletop calendar tools

### Takeaway
Every tool examined keeps exactly one clock per game world; none found supports per-party or per-token time. The nearest thing is Simple Calendar's multiple calendars, each of which keeps its own date and time, but only one is active and activating it overwrites world time for everyone.

### Cited Findings
- Foundry core: world time is a single value, "the amount of seconds elapsed since the beginning of the world's time", stored in one world setting, with two methods to change it (advance and set) (search snippets). — [Foundry community wiki, time API](https://foundryvtt.wiki/en/development/api/time); [Foundry API, GameTime](https://foundryvtt.com/api/v14/classes/foundry.helpers.GameTime.html)
- Simple Calendar ties itself to that one value through an integration setting (Self, Third Party Module, Mixed, None); the default "Mixed" lets it set world time and reflect changes made by other modules (search snippet). — [Simple Calendar docs, General Settings](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/foundryvtt-simple-calendar@2.4.18/simple-calendar-docs/docs/calendar-configuration/general-settings.md)
- Simple Calendar multiple calendars: viewing another calendar shows "the current date and time for that calendar"; only the active calendar's date and time can be changed; making a calendar active sets world time to that calendar's timestamp, and the docs warn that timed effects or conditions may then expire early or last longer than intended. One active calendar applies to all connected players. — [Simple Calendar docs, Switching Calendars](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/foundryvtt-simple-calendar@2.4.18/simple-calendar-docs/docs/using-sc/switching-calendars.md)
- Simple Calendar Reborn is a fork maintained for Foundry 13 and later; About Time Next is a rebuilt fork of about-time providing timed events and macro scheduling on the world clock; Seasons & Stars is a newer calendar module for v13 and later. None advertises separate clocks per party (search snippets from package listings). — [Simple Calendar Reborn](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/foundryvtt-simple-calendar-reborn); [About Time Next](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/about-time-next); [Seasons & Stars](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/seasons-and-stars)
- JD Easy Timekeeping tracks time independently of Foundry's world time (older listing), which shows a module can keep its own clock, but still one clock (search snippet). — [Foundry Hub, JD Easy Timekeeping](https://foundryvtt-hub.com/package/jd-easytimekeeping)
- Party-oriented modules deal with tokens, not time: Crunch My Party collapses groups of tokens into a single party token (search snippet). — [Crunch My Party](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/crunch-my-party)

### Inferences
- The tools encode the default tabletop assumption that the table is one party at one time. The Simple Calendar warning about active effects expiring wrongly when the active calendar is switched is the exact failure a naive "swap the world clock per party" design would hit: any duration stored as an absolute world timestamp breaks when the clock is repointed. Durations in a split-time engine must be stored against the clock of the thing they belong to (a character or a place), not against one global value.
- There is no prior art in mainstream VTT tooling to borrow for per-party time; the tabletop practice sections (1 and 2) are the precedent, not the software.

### Gaps
- Roll20: search summaries described a single-date "Calendar" API script, a "Multi-World Calendar" script, a newer "Chronicle" script with one "Featured Date", and a forum thread titled "Tracking separate calendars for multiple players" in which a GM running one-on-one sessions needed a date per character and was pointed to storing one date per character plus a campaign-level "mule" date that advances only when a character passes it. I could not confirm the thread permalinks (the one I opened was a different thread), so none of this is cited; it should be treated as unverified.
- Fantasy Grounds calendar, Small Time, and the Calendar/Weather module were not researched to source level before the search budget ran out. I have no evidence that any of them supports more than one clock.
- GitHub issue trackers for Simple Calendar were not searched for per-party time requests.

## 5. Text role-play communities: MUSH, MUX, MOO, play-by-post, Discord

### Takeaway
Text communities do not solve the problem mechanically; they solve it socially: a game-wide ratio (usually 1:1) fixes the date, each scene sets its own time of day and runs at its own pace, and "backscenes" fill in what was missed, under the one hard convention that a backscene may not establish anything that contradicts what has already been played later. Forum play-by-post goes further ("liquid" or "fluid" time, one character in many threads at once, each player keeping a personal timeline), and accepts unresolved discrepancies.

### Cited Findings

**Game-wide ratio (who advances time: the wall clock)**
- "Most MUSHes generally run on a 1:1 time ratio." "For every day that passes in the real world, one day passes in the game world as well." By contrast, "In a forum game, time passes when you say it passes." — [AresMUSH, MUSH 101: Time and Space](https://aresmush.com/mush-101/time-and-space.html)
- Trade-offs by ratio. 1-to-1: "it's easy to remember the current IC dates and times", but it "may be too quick for roleplaying out some scenes since people can't be around 24/7" and long things (pregnancies, training) take as long as in life. 4-to-1: "Long-term plots can come to fruition in a reasonable RL time", but "You really have to rely on +time to keep track of things", a scene split across several real nights can leave you "12 days 'out of synch' with IC time" and "you have to retroacively handle other things"; "it's not for the faint of heart." 1-to-4: "There's less retro RP because scenes can be spread out more without getting 'out of synch' with IC time", but "it's sloooow." In-between scales such as 1-to-2 or 3-to-1 exist. — [AresMUSH, "IC vs RL Time"](https://aresmush.com/articles/ic-vs-rl-time.html)
- A current policy example: "City of Hope MUSH does not utilize time compression or expansion: it runs in real time", and "'+time' will show you what that is"; game time even advances through server outages. — [City of Hope MUSH, Policies](https://cityofhopemush.net/index.php/Policies)

**Scene-local time**
- "Although days generally run on 1:1 time, time within the day is more flexible." "Players will usually establish a time-of-day at the beginning of a scene." "Within the scene, time will pass organically, no matter what the RL wall clock says." — [AresMUSH, Time and Space](https://aresmush.com/mush-101/time-and-space.html)
- Intersecting scenes are reconciled by hand: "determining the actual IC time of a scene may require hand waving, especially when scenes intersect"; players whose scene assumes uninterrupted events should "make this clear up front" and "Consider pro-actively informing staff of your intentions, especially if it crosses multiple IC days." — [City of Hope MUSH, Policies](https://cityofhopemush.net/index.php/Policies)

**Absent players and offline characters**
- "Generally, nobody can play your character when you're offline"; "other players should leave the door open for you to come up with reasons why your character wasn't around for a scene." — [AresMUSH, Time and Space](https://aresmush.com/mush-101/time-and-space.html)
- "There is no correct assumption about what a character has been doing while the player was away from the game"; the safe default is that the character was busy and everything is normal; after about three months without contact it is generally safe to handwave that a relationship ended off camera. — [City of Hope MUSH, Policies](https://cityofhopemush.net/index.php/Policies)
- Names for the unavailable-character state: "Offstage, In Pocket, Abstraction Land, Backgrounded" (STD); one game leaves a logged-off character on the grid "as a sleeping object" and offers a PAUSE command by which "your character gets put into a metaphorical bubble so you can go do RL stuff"; complaint from a player: "things were publicly done with my character's sleeping body that I'm totally unaware of" (Aria). — [Brand MU Day, Silent Heaven thread](https://brandmu.day/post/16258)

**Backscenes and what may be established in them**
- "MUSH players can do a flashback scene (called a backscene) to flesh out something that happened in the past." "Backscenes are commonly used to fill in gaps when players couldn't be online together." The constraint: "You can't die in a backscene last week if you've already done other things this week." "Both backscenes and async RP require you to tread carefully to avoid continuity glitches." "Players need to work together to ensure the story stays consistent (within reason)." — [AresMUSH, Time and Space](https://aresmush.com/mush-101/time-and-space.html)
- Lexicon from another game: a backscene is "A scene covering prior events or one that had to be paused and picked up later", and because it happens in the past "they are usually closed scenes" (limited to named characters). — [Creation's Edge MUSH, Newbie Guide](https://creationsedgemush.wikidot.com/newbie-guide)
- Asynchronous scenes exist but are bounded by the running clock: "Weeks-long async scenes, as you might find in forum RP, are far less common on MUSHes because the clock is running." — [AresMUSH, Time and Space](https://aresmush.com/mush-101/time-and-space.html)

**Timestop**
- The term exists in MUSH culture in connection with turn-based, human-moderated combat: MUSHes with coded combat "implement a non-time-sensitive, turn-based combat system", and a 2005 MUD-Dev poster is "familiar with the concept of MUSH 'TimeStops'"; the human-moderated process is "fantastic for roleplaying and immersion" but "took a lot of time and contained very few 'game' qualities." — [MUD-Dev, October 2005](https://mud-dev.zer7.com/2005/10/32546)

**Forum play-by-post and Discord**
- "Liquid" or "fluid" time is defined as entering a single character into multiple topics at once while that character's own history still runs in a set order; players commonly keep a personal timeline, ordered by when they joined each thread or by posting date; some boards require each thread to state time and place; some hold firmly that characters cannot be in different places at the same time; one Star Wars board's staff position is that players cannot dictate the timeline of threads, so discrepancies should usually be ignored rather than argued over (all search snippets; pages blocked the fetch). — [Star Wars RP: Chaos, "Fluid time and how to deal with it"](https://www.starwarsrp.net/threads/fluid-time-and-how-to-deal-with-it.133655); [Star Wars RP: Chaos, "Personal timelines"](https://www.starwarsrp.net/threads/personal-timelines.96382); [RPG Initiative, "Linear or liquid"](https://rpginitiative.com/topic/10729-linear-or-liquid/)
- A Discord rule that takes the opposite, strict line: all activity in a Discord channel is considered to be happening concurrently, so a character may only be active in one Discord scene at a time (search snippet; page refused the fetch). — [Bravo Fleet wiki, Guide: Discord RP](https://wiki.bravofleet.com/index.php/Guide:Discord_RP)

### Inferences
- The MUSH convention "you can't die in a backscene last week if you've already done other things this week" is the general form of the rule the CRPG needs for any catch-up play by a lagging character: actions taken in a character's past relative to the frontier may add facts but may not change any fact that later play has already depended on. The stricter tabletop version (section 1, "no back-dated orders") bans such play outright. An engine can sit between the two by allowing lagging play only where it cannot touch state that has a later recorded reader.
- Scene-local time under a fixed date is a workable abstraction of "seconds or days per action": the MUSH date is coarse and shared, the minute hand belongs to the scene. That suggests two granularities for the engine as well, a coarse shared quantity that must agree when characters meet and a fine local one that need not.
- Liquid time is per-character time with no global ordering at all and no enforcement; it works only because nothing numeric depends on the order. With engine-owned healing, rent and timers it does not fit, but it documents what players tolerate: personal continuity that makes sense matters much more to them than global consistency (the same point the Alexandrian makes in section 1).
- The City of Hope absence defaults ("busy, everything normal") and the Ashen Marches rule ("assumed to have remained in town") are the same solution to the absent character: a declared default activity that costs nothing to narrate. An engine needs the equivalent default for slack that a player does not choose to spend.
- The "sleeping body" complaint is a warning about leaving an idle character's body in the shared world as an object others can act upon.

### Gaps
- I found no primary policy file defining "timestop" or "time bubble" as a staff ruling that freezes one scene while the grid moves on, although the practice is widely referred to in MU* culture; the only citation is a passing 2005 reference. Likewise no primary source was found for +events calendar policy, for staff-declared time skips, or for how staff propagate a plot that one group has advanced. Electric Soup and other archive sites were not reached.
- MOO-specific conventions were not found.
- The forum play-by-post and Discord findings are snippet-level only because the sites blocked automated fetches.
- No source found on how large Discord storytelling servers date and order many concurrent scenes beyond the one-scene-at-a-time rule above.

## 6. Published systems with explicit multi-group or troupe time rules

### Takeaway
Published systems mostly avoid different dates by using a coarse shared quantum (Ars Magica's season, Pendragon's year, Errant's month-long downtime turn, Blades' downtime phase, Traveller's week in jump): within a quantum order does not matter, and everyone reconciles at its boundary. The only explicit catch-up procedure found for a character who fell behind is Pendragon's solo scenario per missed year; none of the systems lets groups sit at different dates and reconcile later.

### Cited Findings

**Ars Magica (seasons, troupe play)**
- Troupe-style play means each player has several characters used at different times and for different missions. — [Wikipedia, Troupe system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troupe_system)
- The season is the accounting unit: Hermetic laboratory work "always takes an entire, actual season", and magi have "four genuinely free seasons per year" (search snippet). — [Project Redcap, ArM5 Chapter Eight: Laboratory](https://www.redcap.org/page/Ars_Magica_5E_Standard_Edition,_Chapter_Eight:_Laboratory)
- Interruption tolerance as players state the rule: "You can miss up to 10 days from lab without penalty. You can miss up to 10 more but at a penalty of -2 to lab total per day. Over 20 missed and you can't do it" (search snippet of a forum post, not the rulebook). — [Atlas Games forum](https://forum.atlas-games.com/t/multiple-non-lab-total-lab-activities/3926)
- Overlap is normal: characters "will frequently find themselves having adventures during different sessions that occur at the same time period in-game because character advancement is seasonal" (search snippet). — [Atlas Games forum, Heir of Laimunt Valley](https://forum.atlas-games.com/t/about-the-ars-magica-5th-edition-the-heir-of-laimunt-valley-category/170155/3)
- Practitioner advice on long journeys and skips: "All trose years long skips in the timeline, long trips far away from the covenant, etc should be done by mutual consent" [sic]; "Use the seasons system instead of trying to calculate everything properly"; "The most valuable resource of this game is time itself"; "Let them go for their long walk of adventure", and on return the covenant's events during the absence become new problems (Toronus). — [Atlas Games forum, "Tight timelines and long journeys in troupe-style play"](https://forum.atlas-games.com/t/tight-timelines-and-long-journeys-in-troupe-style-play/171819)

**Pendragon (the year and the Winter Phase)**
- The Winter Phase is when "the Players update their characters to reflect the experiences of the past year"; each checked statistic gets one roll per year. Catch-up: solo scenarios are "particularly important if a Player was absent for a session", and "even a knight who missed the last game year has a chance to increase their Skills." — [Pendragon wiki, The Winter Phase](https://pendragonwiki.chaosium.com/rules/the-winter-phase.html)
- A player who missed one or more game years may attempt a standard solo scenario for each year missed (search snippet). — [Pendragon wiki, Solo Scenarios](https://pendragonwiki.chaosium.com/rules/solo-scenarios.html)
- Forum view that every character needs a Winter Phase every year regardless of attendance, because of aging, stable and child survival rolls (search snippet). — [Chaosium Pendragon forum archive, "Winter Phase for absent players"](https://greathall.chaosium.com/Pendragon%20Forum%20Archive/index.php/t-2516.html)

**Errant (downtime turns)**
- A downtime turn is "By default, assumed to take about a month." "During a Downtime Turn, Errants specify what action they are taking in the period of time between adventures"; one action each; an Errant may give up the action to assist another (+1). Procedure: "The Guide declares the start of a Downtime Turn; the timekeeper notes down which number Downtime Turn this is", and later "Guide advances the in-game calendar by one month (or appropriate amount)." "Repeat steps 1-11 until the Company no longer wishes to take Downtime Turns." — [Errant SRD, Downtime Turns](https://funkaoshi.github.io/errantsrd/SRD/11-downtime-turns.html)
- Engine-like billing per turn: "When a Downtime Turn ends, whatever remaining money an Errant has is halved to pay for lifestyle expenses"; "If the Company is taking multiple Downtime Turns in a row, they only pay for lifestyle expenses on the last Downtime Turn they take"; "At the start of every Downtime Turn, the remaining amount owed by the Company doubles"; "When a Downtime Turn begins ... all Company members restore HP up to their maximum." — [Errant SRD, Downtime Turns](https://funkaoshi.github.io/errantsrd/SRD/11-downtime-turns.html)

**Blades in the Dark and Band of Blades (phases instead of dates)**
- After a score the GM resolves payoff, heat and entanglements, then "Each PC has time for two downtime activities", only one when the crew is at war; each additional activity costs 1 coin or 1 rep; "Only activities that are on the list are limited" (search snippets of the SRD text). — [Blades in the Dark SRD](https://bladesinthedark.com/node/1); [Roll20 compendium, Downtime](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Rules:Downtime)
- Band of Blades alternates a campaign phase (Legion roles choose missions, spend resources, advance on the map) and a mission phase; time clocks are the main pressure, and staying in one place too long can lose the campaign by filling them (search snippet of a review). — [Gnome Stew, Band of Blades review](https://gnomestew.com/band-of-blades-review/)

**Traveller (the week in jump)**
- Classic Traveller Book 2 as quoted: "making any jump takes about one week, regardless of the distance travelled"; a ship in jump is outside normal space and incommunicado (search snippets). — [Freelance Traveller](https://freelancetraveller.com/features/rules/tech/tweakjd.html); [Traveller wiki, Travelling in Charted Space](https://wiki.travellerrpg.com/Travelling)

**Old-school downtime and megadungeon open tables**
- See section 1 for "every session begins and ends outside the dungeon" and section 2 for the weekly downtime turn house rule; Errant above is the fullest published "downtime turn" procedure found.

### Inferences
- Coarse quanta are a consistency device: Ars Magica never asks which of two same-season adventures happened first, and Pendragon never asks about order within a year. Reconciliation is trivial because the boundary is the only shared instant. The cost is that actions measured in seconds and actions measured in days cannot both be first-class, which the CRPG requires. A hybrid is implied: fine time within a party, with a coarser boundary (a day, a rest, a return to a safe place) as the only instant at which separated parties must agree.
- Ars Magica's interruption allowance (lose up to ten days of a season without penalty) is a published tolerance band: small divergences between characters are simply ignored below a threshold. An engine could use the same idea so that parties a few minutes or hours apart meet without any reconciliation step.
- Pendragon's "one solo scenario per missed year" is the cleanest published catch-up: the lagging character does not get a free fast-forward; it gets one bounded, rule-resolved activity per missed quantum. That is slack spent in priced units, the same shape as the per-character-clock campaign in section 1.
- Errant shows that billing can be attached to the turn boundary rather than to elapsed days (lifestyle paid once at the end of a run of turns, debts doubling per turn), which sidesteps the question of what a rent meter does while two parties are at different dates: charge at the synchronisation boundary, per character, for the quanta that character crossed.
- Blades limits downtime by count, not by time; it is a precedent for the engine pricing between-adventure activity in "slots" instead of days if day accounting proves too fiddly, but it gives no help with a persistent calendar.
- Traveller's fixed week is a design that makes every party's big time-skips the same size, so groups that jump the same number of times stay aligned without tracking. Uniform skip sizes are a cheap way to keep split parties near each other in time.

### Gaps
- Mausritter's rest and between-session procedures, hexcrawl watch procedures, ACKS and other "domain turn" rules, and Necropraxis or Delta's D&D Hotspot posts on downtime turns were not sourced before the search budget ran out.
- Rulebook text for Ars Magica, Pendragon ("one adventure per year" as a stated assumption), Blades in the Dark and Band of Blades was not quoted from the books; the Blades and Band of Blades items are snippet-level, and the Band of Blades community thread on time refused the connection.
- A Traveller referee thread about independent player groups falling "out of sync" (one group spending weeks of real time on a few game days) appeared in search results, but I could not tie it to a URL and found no answer in it.
- I found no published system that explicitly permits groups to hold different dates and gives a procedure for reconciling them; absence of evidence here is limited by the breadth of the search.

## 7. Design writing: time as a resource each character spends versus time as a shared calendar; retcon budgets and continuity repair

### Takeaway
Explicit design writing on this distinction is thin and mostly practitioner-level: the clearest statements are the per-character-clock campaign's "opportunity cost that can only be paid in advance", the BrOSR writers' argument for one shared calendar, and Living Greyhawk's stated purpose for time units. No source was found that uses the phrase "retcon budget" or defines a quantified allowance for continuity repair; repair in practice is ad hoc (staff decide order, ignore discrepancies, declare the monster was elsewhere, or force a synchronisation).

### Cited Findings

**Time as a per-character resource**
- Per-character clocks with slack, and the principle behind forbidding retroactive use: "It's an opportunity cost that can only be paid in advance to be meaningful." — [Erin, Campaign News December 17](https://erin1478.blogspot.com/2025/12/campaign-news-december-17.html)
- Organised play's statement of the idea: time units "represent the how much your PC can do in a year". — [Archclericy of Veluna FAQ](https://www.veluna.gilkison.net/whatis/faq.html)
- Ars Magica practitioner: "The most valuable resource of this game is time itself." — [Atlas Games forum](https://forum.atlas-games.com/t/tight-timelines-and-long-journeys-in-troupe-style-play/171819)
- Why time must be tracked at all: "Time tracking becomes really quite important when the campaign is dealing with either regular upkeep" and "Time is really what forces the players to make decisions about planning an expedition" (Yora). — [EN World, "Time in the Campaign"](https://www.enworld.org/threads/time-in-the-campaign.690348/post-8715428)
- The DMG's cautionary example as retold: one group "lolly-gags around, then misses out on a chance to gain XP and treasure" "because another group played through while they were goofing off". — [Stephen Smith, "Timekeeping in D&D RPGs"](https://stephensmith.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-d-and-d-rpgs)

**Time as one shared calendar**
- The argument for it: tracking who arrives first is simplified by one calendar, and per-character timelines put "too many disparate elements moving at the same time"; without strict timekeeping time skips become trivial and a campaign can gain many levels in little game time. — [Border Baronies](https://borderbaronies.bearblog.dev/emergent-experiences-or-how-i-learned-to-relinquish-control-and-embrace-the-brosr-strict-timekeeping/)
- A server's flat statement: "There are no character-specific calendars or return dates." — [Ashen Marches wiki](https://www.westmarches.games/communities/the-ashen-marches/wiki/time-tracking-in-the-ashen-marches)
- The counter-position that the ratio is the timekeeper's free choice: "The DM as Timekeeper can make almost any T:T* ratio work in the game" (commenter); "Some sessions are longer than others. Sometimes, you press pause. Sometimes you don't." — [Stephen Smith](https://stephensmith.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-d-and-d-rpgs)

**Continuity repair as actually practised**
- Decide order after the fact: "the GMs determine which adventure happened first." — [Ashen Marches wiki](https://www.westmarches.games/communities/the-ashen-marches/wiki/time-tracking-in-the-ashen-marches)
- Order by outcome and quarantine gains: "If you die in the PBP, then any voice session is assumed to have run some time before the PBP." — [CWM Player's Guide](https://gmbinder.com/share/-LAmPCMCp-4L2HdO0mUT)
- Avoid the collision: "it is sometimes necessary to adventure elsewhere to avoid creating a paradox"; limit how far forward one session may jump. — [Primeval Patterns Part 2](https://primevalpatterns.substack.com/p/timekeeping-in-ttrpgs-part-2)
- Force alignment: "It's possible that I will need to force clock synchronizations at some point." — [Erin, Campaign News December 17](https://erin1478.blogspot.com/2025/12/campaign-news-december-17.html)
- Accept local consistency only: "every individual character will have their own personal continuity that will make sense"; one commenter picks a "canonical" outcome and narrates "the outcome of the previous investigation" at the start of the next session. — [The Alexandrian, "Opening Your Game Table" (comments)](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1223/roleplaying-games/opening-your-game-table)
- Constrain the past: "You can't die in a backscene last week if you've already done other things this week." — [AresMUSH, Time and Space](https://aresmush.com/mush-101/time-and-space.html)
- Ignore it: on one large forum, discrepancies between threads should usually be ignored rather than argued over (search snippet). — [Star Wars RP: Chaos](https://www.starwarsrp.net/threads/fluid-time-and-how-to-deal-with-it.133655)

### Inferences

Cross-model comparison (my synthesis of the cited material in all sections):

| Model | Clock owner | Lagging or absent character | Character who ran ahead | Contradiction control | Main complaint | Fit for the CRPG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 time with time jail | Real calendar | Idles in real time; downtime by orders | Locked until the calendar arrives | Shared calendar; avoid places with a recorded future | Locked out; needs many characters and players | Poor as a whole; the "busy until T" lock on a committed character is reusable |
| Per-character clocks with frontier | Each character; frontier = most advanced | Has slack, spends it forward on downtime up to the frontier; no back-dating | Defines the frontier; others catch up to meet | Equal dates to meet; forced sync as last resort | Referee load and error risk | Best match; the engine removes the stated weakness |
| Single shared date, sequential expeditions | Each expedition advances it for all | Deemed to have waited in town | Cannot happen (no overlap) | Staff rule on order | Requires sequential play | Simple but ages everyone whenever anyone skips; workable only if that is acceptable |
| Shared calendar table with busy marks | DMs fill a log | Earns 1 downtime point per idle day | Blocked from events dated earlier | One event per date; never back in time | Human log errors | The two constraints are directly enforceable |
| Downtime-day currency (AL, PFS, many servers) | None | Nothing happens | Not applicable | Episodic play, no shared state | Edge-case confusion; little meaning | Good for pricing activities; no help for a persistent world clock |
| Annual time budget (Living Greyhawk) | Real year; per-character ledger | Unspent time simply lapses | Out of time units: unplayable until new year | Only the year is shared | Lockout, big sinks, flat upkeep, paperwork | Ledger and per-unit upkeep reusable; the cap is not |
| MUSH 1:1 with scenes and backscenes | Wall clock for the date; scene for the hour | Assumed busy; backscene to fill in | Scene "out of synch" handled retroactively | Social; backscene may not contradict later play | Continuity glitches; things done to idle characters | The backscene constraint is the key rule to encode |
| Liquid time (forum play) | Each player | Not applicable | Not applicable | Personal timelines; discrepancies ignored | Disorder | Does not fit engine-owned numbers |
| Coarse quantum (season, year, month turn) | Table, at boundaries | One catch-up activity per missed quantum (Pendragon) | Waits for the boundary | Order within a quantum is irrelevant | Cannot express fine time | Useful as the synchronisation boundary and billing point |

- "Time as a resource each character spends" and "time as a shared calendar" are not exclusive in the best-fitting model: the frontier is a shared calendar, and the distance from each character to it is that character's resource. Living Greyhawk is the pure resource form (no relative dates at all); the Ashen Marches is the pure calendar form (no per-character quantity at all); the per-character-clock campaign sits between and is the only one that supports split parties acting at different speeds in one persistent world.
- A "retcon budget" as a named, quantified mechanism appears to be absent from tabletop practice. What exists instead are four qualitative repair moves: reorder (staff or rule decides which happened first), relocate (the contested thing was elsewhere or the party must go elsewhere), quarantine (gains do not cross an overlap), and resynchronise (forced alignment). An engine could implement all four deterministically; the tabletop record suggests players accept them when they are stated in advance as rules and resent them when improvised.
- Everything in the tabletop record that goes wrong goes wrong at one of three points: a human forgets or mis-enters a date; two groups touch the same place at inconsistent dates; or a player is told they cannot play. The first is solved by the engine by construction. The second needs an explicit rule about places and shared objects. The third is a design choice the critics' testimony argues strongly against.

### Gaps
- No essay-level design writing was found (Prismatic Wasteland, Necropraxis, Delta's D&D Hotspot, Grognardia were on the suggested list but not reached before the search budget ran out) that contrasts per-character time with a shared calendar in those terms. The contrast above is assembled from practitioner posts.
- No source found for "retcon budget" or any quantified continuity-repair allowance in shared campaigns.
- No source found that analyses causality between groups formally (for example in terms of which facts a group has observed); the nearest is the single sentence that a paradox needs events to "collide in time and in space".
