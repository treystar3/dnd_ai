# Game time in co-op RPGs and multiplayer sandbox games when players are apart, skip time, or are offline

Reading notes on evidence quality. "Fetched" means the page or source file itself was read. "Search summary" means the claim comes from a search engine's digest of the linked page and the page itself could not be opened (the KSP forum, the Stardew wiki, the Arelith wiki and several Steam threads returned 403 or empty bodies). Hosting-company guides are marked as such; they are weak sources. "Fit" in the Inferences blocks means fit for a host-authoritative co-op text RPG where one typed action can take seconds or days, players split up and reunite in one persistent world, and there is no player cap.

## 1. Larian's engine: Divinity: Original Sin 1/2 and Baldur's Gate 3

### Takeaway
Larian runs one real-time world clock and carves out local turn-based bubbles: only creatures near the fight are frozen into turns, everyone else (including other players) keeps acting in real time, so a round that is nominally 6 seconds can last many real minutes while a distant partner crosses the map. Time skipping does not exist in DOS2; in BG3 it exists only as the long rest, which is a whole-party, everyone-accepts, everyone-is-teleported-to-camp event, and it is the part players report getting stuck on.

### Cited Findings
- DOS1 (Enhanced Edition): combat is turn-based only for those in it; "the other party members can still walk around freely, start their own fights, or even join that character while combat is in progress" (search summary of the wiki page). [Fextralife DOS wiki, Combat](https://divinityoriginalsin.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat)
- DOS1 co-op as observed by a journalist: "Combat in Divinity is turn-based, but a co-op partner sees things happening in real time"; the partner wandered up and watched the fight from outside it (search summary). [PC Gamer, "Now Playing: co-op calamity in Divinity: Original Sin"](https://pcgamer.com/now-playing-co-op-calamity-in-divinity-original-sin)
- DOS2: "The game is played in real-time, except during combat... When combat is over, the game returns to real-time" (search summary). [Fextralife DOS2 wiki, Combat](https://divinityoriginalsin2.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat)
- DOS2 late joining, forum post only (user Blevak, 21 Sep 2017): "when you join in combat you join in at the very end of the current turn", listed by the poster as an exploit ("Escapist Infinites": leave and rejoin). Not confirmed by Larian. [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/435150/discussions/0/1495615865213439209)
- I found no source confirming for DOS2 online co-op specifically that a non-combatant can shop or talk elsewhere during a partner's fight; it is confirmed for DOS1 and consistent with every DOS2 description found. See Gaps.
- BG3 turn-based mode is spatial, not global: "Entering turn-based mode by any means freezes events for all creatures and objects in combat-range proximity of the character(s) it is activated with, while events continue as normal outside of this range" (search summary quoting the wiki). [bg3.wiki, Turn-based mode](https://bg3.wiki/wiki/Turn-based_mode)
- BG3: a party member who moves far enough away "can flee combat with that character, which ends turn-based mode for them, while others remain in combat"; and "Entering dialogue does not toggle on turn-based mode, allowing real-time events to continue occurring in the vicinity" (search summary). [bg3.wiki, Turn-based mode](https://bg3.wiki/wiki/Turn-based_mode)
- BG3 simultaneous turns (Larian, Early Access era): "in multiplayer, when your avatars and companions are next to each other in the turn order players can simultaneously control characters." [Larian, "A Little About Combat & Stealth"](https://baldursgate3.game/news/a-little-about-combat-stealth_3)
- The complaint this answered: in DOS2-style co-op "you spend most of a fight doing nothing, just waiting for your turn" (search summary; the article body could not be fetched). [PC Gamer, "Baldur's Gate 3's simultaneous turn-based combat is a blessing for multiplayer"](https://www.pcgamer.com/baldurs-gate-3s-simultaneous-turn-based-combat-is-a-blessing-for-multiplayer)
- The contradiction players notice, forum post only: "A turn has 6s.. so if you engage combat with just one character, the others need to get in turn based mode or they will be traveling in time or getting supervelocity" (search summary of a Steam thread; thread body not retrievable). [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/3802776070751871183)
- BG3 long rest moves the whole party: "Active party members also move here once a long rest is started" (search summary). [bg3.wiki, Camp](https://bg3.wiki/wiki/Camp)
- BG3 long rest requires out-of-combat state and costs 40 camp supplies (search summaries of guides). [Fextralife BG3 wiki, Rest](https://baldursgate3.wiki.fextralife.com/Rest)
- BG3 long-rest consent stalls, forum post only (Feb 2024): the group decided to long rest and the game sat on "waiting for all players to accept" and timed out even after all had accepted. [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/0/6292161380820233297)
- Larian's Hotfix 4 (16 Aug 2023) fixed "players getting stuck mid-Long-Rest due to companions not waking up" (search summary). [Larian, Hotfix #4](https://baldursgate3.game/news/hotfix-4-now-live_85)
- A Patch 6 era honour-mode report says a joiner was refused with a message that they could not join "until the host takes a long rest" (search summary; one of several Steam threads returned, exact thread not verified). [Steam discussions, BG3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1086940/discussions/3/6292161380813262747)
- Patch 2 added a turn notification naming whose turn it is, and Patch 8 added an on-screen indicator that you are in turn-based mode (search summaries of patch notes). [Fextralife BG3 wiki, Patch Notes](https://baldursgate3.wiki.fextralife.com/Patch+Notes)

### Inferences
- Larian's answer to "players in different time modes" is to not track elapsed game time at all outside rests. There is no world clock that a 6-second round or a 10-minute walk debits, so the "time travelling" a Steam poster describes has no mechanical consequence. That escape is not available to a design in which every action consumes measured game time.
- The only place Larian has a real time skip (BG3 long rest), it falls back to the strictest rule in this whole survey: every player consents, every character is physically gathered, and the day ends for all at once. The reported pain is not the rule itself but its failure modes (consent prompt hanging, one player not ready, joins blocked until the day boundary).
- Spatial scoping of the turn bubble ("combat-range proximity") is the reusable idea: a time mode belongs to a place, and entering the place means entering its mode. Late joiners enter at a turn boundary, which is also the natural merge point for a text game.
- Fit: partial. The place-scoped bubble and join-at-boundary rule transfer well. The "no clock outside rests" premise does not, and the all-must-consent rest becomes unworkable with no player cap.

### Gaps
- No primary Larian statement was found on what a DOS2 player outside combat may do during a partner's fight, or on any time cost of it. The PC Gamer article bodies and the BG3 Steam threads could not be fetched in full.
- No source found on whether BG3 lets one player go to camp alone while others continue (it is widely described by players as possible, with only the long rest itself requiring everyone), nor on Larian's own reasoning for "everyone ends the day together".
- No source found on BG3 patch changes specifically to the long-rest consent flow after Hotfix 4.

## 2. Other co-op RPGs: Solasta, Pathfinder, Neverwinter Nights, Wasteland 3, Outward

### Takeaway
Party-based co-op RPGs avoid the problem by keeping the party a single unit for anything that moves the clock (Solasta: host moves the party on the world map; Wasteland 3: splitting is allowed only "within the same level"). Neverwinter Nights persistent worlds are the one family with many independent players on one always-running clock, and they solved rest by turning it from a time skip into a cooldown measured on that clock.

### Cited Findings
**Solasta: Crown of the Magister (co-op added with Lost Valley, April 2022) and Solasta II (co-op in Early Access Update 1, September 2026)**
- Co-op for up to four arrived with the Lost Valley DLC and works in both campaigns (search summary). [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/solasta-crown-of-the-magister/lost-valley-dlc-dnd-rpg)
- Session start is a ready check: "Once all players click ready (bottom right), you can start the game", and the host assigns characters to joiners (search summary). [Tactical Adventures, "Multiplayer Beta Weekend is live"](https://www.solasta-game.com/news/150-multiplayer-beta-weekend-is-live)
- Solasta II: movement on the world map stays with the host, so the party travels as a unit; dialogue choices go to a vote in which "majority wins, timer applies the result at zero" (search summary of a secondary guide). [AllThings.How, Solasta II Update 1](https://allthings.how/solasta-ii-update-1-how-co-op-multiplayer-and-the-warlock-work/)
- Solasta II co-op lets players "vote on important game decisions" (search summary). [PCGamesN, Solasta 2 multiplayer update](https://www.pcgamesn.com/solasta-2/multiplayer-update)

**Pathfinder: Kingmaker and Wrath of the Righteous**
- Neither game has official multiplayer. A 2021 Steam thread asking for a co-op mod was answered with doubt that modders could build one, citing the lack of a Kingmaker precedent (forum post only). [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1184370/discussions/0/4939872515519106109)

**Neverwinter Nights (persistent worlds and co-op modules)**
- "Resting restrictions are somewhat common in some modules, particularly persistent worlds", typically a limit on how often a character may rest, sometimes a cap on hit points restored (search summary). [NWN wiki, Rest](https://nwn.fandom.com/wiki/Rest)
- Time scale is a per-server choice. Arelith "previously ran a 10:1 time scale before it was changed to the current 3:1 time scale", and the page notes other persistent worlds and single player differ (search summary; page returned 403 on fetch). [Arelith wiki, Time](https://wiki.nwnarelith.com/Time)
- A Bioware designer's 2004 post about one official module: on Normal difficulty "the delay between rests is three minutes", on Core D&D six minutes, i.e. rest gated by real-time delay (search summary). [GameBanshee, NWN chat log](https://www.gamebanshee.com/ky38)
- ALFA (a NWN2 persistent world) debated changing the rest timer rather than the time compression, e.g. "instead of 24 IG hours make it 8" (search summary; fetch timed out). [ALFA forum, time compression announcement](https://alandfaraway.info/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?p=627874)

**Wasteland 3 (two-player co-op, 2020)**
- Director Tim Campbell (Game Informer interview, 19 June 2020, relayed 23 June 2020): "As long as you're within the same level, you can separate and do different things"; "One person can get into a fight while the other person is shopping at a merchant"; and because combat is turn-based, "as soon as one person is in combat, the other player has to be able to merge into that combat when they get near." [GameBanshee, "An Overview of Co-op Features"](http://www.gamebanshee.com/3dhd3)
- Earlier design statement: the squads "can travel and fight together, or they can split up and work independently, which could have consequences for both players" (search summary). [PC Gamer on Fig Update #4](https://www.pcgamer.com/wasteland-3-multiplayer-details-revealed-in-new-fig-update)
- What broke: co-op progression resets were "a well-documented bug... prevalent since its initial release", with the workaround of turning off autosaves and saving manually (search summary). [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/120093/how-to-fix-wasteland-3-co-op-progression-being-reset?amphtml=1)
- Patch 1.1.0 fixed "issues that could cause character and quest progression to be lost"; a later patch blamed "mismatched clients" for most co-op trouble (search summaries). [GameBanshee, Patch 1.1.0](https://gamebanshee.com/344yf); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/new-wasteland-3-patch-resolves-issues-with-co-op-and-beefs-up-polly-and-the-cyborg-chickens)

**Outward (two-player, split-screen or online)**
- Co-op is host plus one guest; the guest brings a character into the host's world (search summary). [Outward wiki mirror, Co-op](https://breezewiki.discard.no/outward/wiki/Co-op)
- A reviewer describes the pair "sleep-scum[ming]" health back together; no source describing the rule for who must sleep was found (search summary). [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/playing-outward-with-a-reckless-co-op-partner-is-a-good-way-to-test-your-relationship)

### Inferences
- The NWN persistent-world pattern is the closest RPG precedent for "no cap on players, one world clock": the clock never jumps for anyone; rest is an activity with a cooldown expressed in game hours, and the server picks the ratio of game to real time. Servers that changed the ratio (Arelith 10:1 to 3:1) were tuning how often day and night and rest cooldowns come round, which shows the ratio is a felt design parameter, not plumbing.
- Wasteland 3's "same level" tether is a cheap consistency device: independent action is allowed only inside one loaded scene, so nothing that moves the world clock (map travel) can be done by half the party.
- Wasteland 3's co-op failures were state replication and save ownership failures, not time paradoxes. A design where one host holds all state avoids that class.
- Fit: NWN's cooldown-on-a-shared-clock fits a no-cap world well but gives up the fiction of a rest actually passing eight hours for the rester. Solasta's and Wasteland 3's single-unit party does not fit a design where players truly split up across the world.

### Gaps
- Solasta 1 multiplayer rest and travel rules (who confirms a long rest or a journey) were not found in any source; only Solasta II's host-controlled world map and dialogue vote were.
- No working co-op mod for Kingmaker or Wrath of the Righteous was found, so there is nothing to report on their rest or world-map time.
- Wasteland 3: no source found on separate simultaneous conversations, on world-map travel rules in co-op, or on playing while the partner is offline.
- NWN: no fetched primary source for the module "minutes per hour" property or DM-client time controls; Arelith's reasons and dates for the 10:1 to 3:1 change were not retrievable.
- Outward's sleep rule in co-op is unconfirmed.

## 3. Survival and sandbox games with one shared clock: Valheim, Minecraft, Terraria, Stardew Valley, Project Zomboid, Don't Starve Together, 7 Days to Die, Palworld, ARK, Rust, Factorio, Satisfactory

### Takeaway
Every game here keeps a single server clock and treats a time skip as a collective act: the default is unanimity (all players in bed), and the consistent player complaint is that one busy or idle player blocks everyone, which developers or modders then relax to a percentage, a vote with a timeout, or a proportional speed-up. Absence is handled separately by choosing between "world pauses when empty", "world runs but your area is unloaded", or "world runs and your stuff is exposed, with protection or decay timers".

### Cited Findings
**Valheim**
- Vanilla rule: "Sleep begins when everyone has laid down in bed"; a forum poster notes one player who stays up building leaves everyone waiting (search summary, forum post). [Quarter to Three forum](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/valheim-a-viking-multiplayer-exploration-and-survival-game-by-coffee-stain/150991?page=26)
- A modder (Feb 2021) called the all-in-bed requirement "a slight anoyance" and patched the server so one player can start sleep. [archevel blog](https://archevel.github.io/valheim/modding/2021/02/16/modding-valheim-dedicated-server.html)
- The community's replacements, in order of sophistication: any single sleeper skips for all ([NowYouSleep](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Hex_Viking/NowYouSleep/)); a ratio of sleepers ([SkipSleep](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/R1NS3/SkipSleep/)); a vote opened once 50% of online players are in bed ([LessSleep](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/LessCx/LessSleep/)); a percentage vote where v1.3.0 added a timeout "so that AFK players can't block sleep" ([SleepSkip](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Azumatt/SleepSkip/changelog)); night speeds up in proportion to the share in bed ([BetterSleepBruh](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/Vapok/BetterSleepBruh), [Ember](https://thunderstore.io/c/valheim/p/expDARE/Ember/)).
- No Iron Gate change to the vanilla rule was found.

**Minecraft**
- Fetched: "To skip the night in multiplayer, all players in the Overworld must be in bed at the same time." "Once all players in a world are asleep, after 5 seconds (100 ticks) the time of day changes to sunrise." The share is set by the game rule `players_sleeping_percentage` (Java) or `playerssleepingpercentage` (Bedrock); above 100, sleeping shows "No amount of rest can pass this night." and night skipping is disabled. Skipping also resets weather. [Minecraft Wiki, Bed](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bed)
- The rule was introduced in Java snapshot 20w51a for 1.17 (search summary of Mojang's tracker and a datapack changelog). [Mojang bug tracker MCPE-172910](https://bugs-legacy.mojang.com/browse/MCPE-172910); [Modrinth, AFK Sleep changelog](https://modrinth.com/datapack/afk-sleep/changelog)
- Example from Mojang's feedback site: with 4 players and the rule at 25, one sleeper is enough (search summary). [Minecraft feedback](https://feedback.minecraft.net/hc/en-us/community/posts/360050355971/comments/360008529912)
- Before 1.17 the same thing was a mod ("Sleepy Head" for 1.7.10) and a staple server plugin; the feedback thread and Bedrock bug reports show it was a long-running request. [CurseForge, Sleepy Head](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/sleepy-head)
- Scope note: only players in the Overworld count; players in the Nether or End neither block nor need to sleep. [Minecraft Wiki, Bed](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bed)

**Terraria**
- Sleeping in a bed makes time pass about five times faster (added in Desktop 1.4.0.1); "Everyone in a multiplayer server must be in a bed to speed up time"; not possible during events or while bosses are alive (search summary). [Terraria Wiki, Sleeping](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Sleeping)
- Enchanted Sundial: any one player can use it; it fast-forwards time at 60x to the next dawn; 7-day cooldown shared by all sundials in the world; "In multiplayer, the Sundial will only count a cooldown day passing if there are players online when the in-game day changes"; bed sleep adds nothing while it is active (search summary). [Terraria Wiki, Time](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Time)

**Stardew Valley (multiplayer added in 1.3, 2018)**
- "In multiplayer, all players must be sleeping simultaneously (or pass out) to progress to the overnight profit breakdown, and all players must click 'ok'... in order to save and start the next day." While in bed, "a dialogue will indicate how many other players are also waiting to end the day." (search summary). [Stardew Valley Wiki, Day Cycle](https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/Day_Cycle)
- A daytime nap raises the sleep confirmation and "all players will see an automatic message announcing the player's intent to sleep" (search summary). [Stardew Valley Wiki, Sleep](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Sleep)
- Sleeping and starting a festival are "decisions made collaboratively by all players by either clicking dialogues or joining areas" (search summary). [Stardew Valley Wiki, Multiplayer](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/multiplayer)
- The day has a hard end: anyone not in bed passes out at 2am, so unanimity is forced by the clock rather than by agreement. [Stardew Valley Wiki, Day Cycle](https://www.stardewvalleywiki.com/Day_Cycle)
- Complaint (2018 beta, forum posts only): the clock keeps running while a player is in inventory, chests or shops, so days feel rushed; one suggestion: "In a two player game the time could move only at half speed if one player is not moving." [Chucklefish forum](https://community.playstarbound.com/threads/multiplayer-suggestion-pause-the-game-time-if-no-player-moves.147446/); [Chucklefish forum, "Changes needed to be made for MP Success"](https://community.playstarbound.com/threads/changes-needed-to-be-made-for-mp-success.146273/)
- The modded answer: Time Master gives the host control of time and offers pause modes including slowing "to half speed in a 2 player game when one player has a chest open" (search summary). [Nexus Mods, Time Master](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/20847)
- Failure mode of the unanimous sleep screen (single user report): pressing a button on "Waiting other players" blacked out all screens and "kinda softlocks everyone until players who weren't asleep leave the game"; said to affect festivals too. [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/413150/discussions/1/4353365446956547262)
- If someone quits before the end-of-day save completes, the day's progress may be lost (hosting-site guide, secondary). [iTechGuides](https://www.itechguides.com/stardew-valley-lag-save-and-co-op-fixes-by-problem)

**Project Zomboid**
- Server options `SleepAllowed` and `SleepNeeded` both default to false; `SleepNeeded` is ignored if `SleepAllowed` is false (hosting guide). [XGamingServer docs](https://xgamingserver.com/docs/project-zomboid/sleep-time-settings)
- Sources conflict on the fast-forward. One hosting wiki: "Time will only speed up if ALL players on the server are sleeping simultaneously", and any player waking returns it to normal. [Supercraft wiki](https://supercraft.host/wiki/project-zomboid/server-sleeping). A Steam post recalls the same from build 40: "If one person has a nightmare, the others will stay asleep, but time goes back to normal speed". [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/3824159062921938381). Other guides say multiplayer sleep does not speed time at all and only removes fatigue. [XGamingServer docs](https://xgamingserver.com/docs/project-zomboid/sleep-time-settings)
- One guide concludes sleep is "best used for small groups only" because everyone must be asleep (search summary). [servers4gamers](https://servers4gamers.com/blog/how-to-enable-sleep-on-a-project-zomboid-server/)

**Don't Starve Together**
- No sleep-to-skip; the clock is the server's. The server setting `pause_when_empty` stops the world when no one is connected; a Klei bug report notes it "is always set to true" in cluster.ini (search summary, forum posts). [Klei bug tracker](https://forums.kleientertainment.com/klei-bug-tracker/dont-starve-together/dedicated-server-self-regen-issue-when-the-last-player-dies-and-leaves-it-as-ghost-r3591/); [Klei forums](https://forums.kleientertainment.com/forums/topic/67652-cave-trouble-in-dedicated/)

**7 Days to Die**
- Sources conflict. Several say an empty server freezes the clock ("no players = server time paused"; "the time/date in the world is PAUSED when no one is on the server"). [7d2d.net FAQ](https://7d2d.net/7-days-to-die-does-the-time-pause-if-no-one-online/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251570/discussions/4/3819662889089014387). A community wiki says dedicated servers "continue running game time even when no players are connected" and there is "no native pause when empty feature". [7d2d community wiki](https://wiki.7d2d.net/hosting/community/how-to-mp-with-friend-with-no-in-game-time-passing-when-no-one-is-playing). No version is given by either side.

**Palworld**
- Sources conflict on offline bases. Hosting guides: pals work "24/7 (as long as chunks are loaded)". [Supercraft guide](https://supercraft.host/wiki/palworld/guide). A gaming site: the game unloads a base when no player is near and "your Pals won't be producing or working at the base at all while you're offline". [GamesFuze](https://gamesfuze.com/guides/palworld-how-to-prevent-your-pals-injuries-and-starving-while-offline/). The most specific account splits by mechanic: timer-based production may be computed from elapsed time on reload, while pathing-based work such as transporting pauses (hosting guide, unverified). [Supercraft guide](https://supercraft.host/wiki/palworld/guide)
- `AutoResetGuildNoOnlinePlayers` can delete a guild and its base after inactivity (hosting guide). [Supercraft guide](https://supercraft.host/wiki/palworld/guide)

**ARK: Survival Evolved and Rust (always-on servers, offline exposure)**
- ARK's native offline raid protection is a server option: `PreventOfflinePvP` with `PreventOfflinePvPInterval` (e.g. 900 seconds after logout before protection starts); protection drops when a tribe member logs in. Guides disagree on whether protection is total or a damage reduction (hosting guides). [BisectHosting](https://help.bisecthosting.com/hc/es-mx/articles/40328851817371-How-to-Set-Up-Offline-PvP-Protection-on-an-ARK-Survival-Evolved-Server); [Supercraft wiki](https://supercraft.host/wiki/ark/ark_pvp_offline_protection/)
- Rust: a structure in range of a stocked Tool Cupboard does not decay, upkeep is drawn from the cupboard, and when it runs empty the base decays; `decay.upkeep_period_minutes` defaults to 1440 (24 real hours) (hosting and community guides). [XGamingServer](https://xgamingserver.com/blog/rust-decay-and-upkeep-guide/); [Corrosion Hour](https://www.corrosionhour.com/rust-decay-upkeep-variable/)

**Factorio and Satisfactory**
- Factorio: `auto_pause` in server-settings.json, "Whether should the server be paused when no players are present", on by default; `--no-auto-pause` keeps it running; `auto_pause_when_players_connect` pauses while someone is joining and catching up (search summary of forum threads and a hosting reference). [Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=586486); [GameServerKings](https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/factorio/factorio-server-settings-json/)
- Satisfactory dedicated servers have an Auto Pause toggle: it "stops the game simulation when no players are connected" and resumes when the first player reconnects (hosting guides). [XGamingServer docs](https://xgamingserver.com/docs/satisfactory/auto-pause)

### Inferences
- The recurring arc is: launch with unanimity, players complain that one person blocks the skip, then relax it. Minecraft took until 1.17 to make it a rule; Valheim's vanilla rule is still unanimity and has at least six mods replacing it; Stardew forces the issue with a hard 2am pass-out. The relaxations found, in increasing fairness: any one player; a fixed share; a vote; a vote with a timeout so idle players count as assent; a speed-up proportional to the share asleep.
- Proportional speed-up (Valheim's BetterSleepBruh and Ember; Stardew's Time Master slowing the clock by the share of players in menus) is the only variant that needs no agreement at all and degrades gracefully with many players. It treats time rate as a continuous function of how many players want time to pass.
- Minecraft scopes the electorate by place: only Overworld players count. That is a small-scale version of "a skip needs the consent of those the skip would affect", and it is the piece most relevant to split parties.
- Terraria's sundial shows the alternative of a unilateral skip rationed by a world-level cooldown rather than by consent.
- These games can afford a global skip because nothing a player does takes long in game time; in a game where one action can take days, "everyone else's clock jumps because I slept" would cancel or overrun the other players' own long actions.
- Absence has three workable settings and each has a named cost: pause when empty (Factorio, Satisfactory, DST: nothing happens to you, but nothing grows either); run but unload (Palworld: inconsistent results between timer-based and simulated work, the source of the conflicting reports); run and expose (ARK, Rust: needs protection windows and decay upkeep, and creates logout-timer exploits).
- Fit: the consent mechanics fit small groups in one place. With no player cap and split parties they only work if the electorate is scoped to a location and if non-voters default to assent after a timeout.

### Gaps
- No official Iron Gate, The Indie Stone, Pocketpair or Fun Pimps statement was found for any of the behaviours above; Project Zomboid's and 7 Days to Die's actual current behaviour is unresolved between conflicting secondary sources.
- The Bedrock version that added the sleeping-percentage rule is unresolved (a search summary says a feedback page lists 1.21.50; not verified). The Java default value of 100 was not confirmed from a fetched source, though "all players in the Overworld" is the stated default behaviour.
- Stardew: whether vanilla has any host pause in multiplayer, exactly which cutscenes or menus pause the clock, and what later versions (1.5, 1.6) changed could not be confirmed; the wiki's Multiplayer page returned 403.
- Enshrouded and Raft: no usable results. ARK and Rust sleeping bodies (the logged-out character remaining in the world and killable) are well known but no citable source was retrieved in this session.

## 4. RimWorld Multiplayer mod: "async time" (per-map clocks)

### Takeaway
With async time on, every map has its own tick counter, speed setting and random-number state, and the world map has another; the world runs at the speed of the fastest unpaused map, and nothing ever brings the clocks back together. Consistency inside a map is kept by deterministic lockstep with commands scheduled per map; consistency between maps at different times is simply not addressed by the time system, and the feature has a standing reputation for bugs (quests, pauses, cooldowns), including a 2023 community warning not to use it.

### Cited Findings
**Architecture (from the mod's source, fetched)**
- Networking is "deterministic lockstep"; the server ticks at about 16.66 ms per tick. [RimWorld Multiplayer dev documentation](https://hackmd.io/@rimworldmultiplayer/dev-general)
- Each map has an `AsyncTimeComp` holding `mapTicks` (its own tick counter), a desired `TimeSpeed`, a `TimeSlower` (forced-normal-speed window), its own tick lists (`tickListNormal`, `tickListRare`, `tickListLong`), its own `randState` seeded from the world seed and the map id, and its own queue of scheduled commands (`cmds`). [AsyncTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimeComp.cs)
- A map tick: swap the global `TickManager` into this map's time (`PreContext`), run `MapPreTick`, `mapTicks++`, tick the map's lists, tick the storyteller, story watcher and (if unpaused) quests, `MapPostTick`, then restore the globals (`PostContext`) and record the map's random state for desync checking. [AsyncTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimeComp.cs)
- The swap is a four-field `TimeSnapshot`: `ticksGameInt`, `curTimeSpeed`, `slower`, `gameStartAbsTick`. `GetAndSetFromMap(map)` returns the previous globals and loads the map's values. In other words, vanilla's single global "now" is replaced by "now, as seen from whichever map is currently being processed". [SetMapTime.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/SetMapTime.cs)
- The same swap is applied around UI and rendering for the map being looked at (map interface, alerts, float menus, hediff tooltips, pawn portraits and tweening, danger rating, sound sustainers, condition causers), with the comment "Set the map time for GUI methods depending on it." It is skipped while the world view is selected. [SetMapTime.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/SetMapTime.cs)
- Things on a map are registered into that map's own tick lists instead of the global ones, and vanilla per-map manager ticks are cancelled unless that map is the one currently being ticked. [AsyncTimePatches.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimePatches.cs)
- Per-map speed multipliers: paused 0, normal 1, fast 3, superfast 6 (12 when "nothing happening" is cached for that map), ultrafast 15. Any pausing session (e.g. a trade or ritual dialog) forces that map to 0. [AsyncTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimeComp.cs)
- The world has its own `AsyncWorldTimeComp` with `worldTicks` and its own `randState`. Its speed is derived, not chosen: with maps present, `DesiredTimeSpeed` is the maximum of the desired speeds of all maps whose actual rate is not zero, else paused. The world follows the fastest running map. [AsyncWorldTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncWorldTimeComp.cs)
- Both the world and each map implement one interface, `ITickable`: `TickableId`, `Cmds`, `TimeToTickThrough`, `DesiredTimeSpeed`, `TickRateMultiplier(speed)`, `Tick()`, `ExecuteCmd(cmd)`. The world's id is -1. [ITickable.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/ITickable.cs)
- Commands are routed to a tickable and executed in its time context; `CommandType.MapTimeSpeed` sets one map's speed and is honoured only when async time is on. [AsyncTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimeComp.cs)

**Who controls time**
- With async time off, speed is a vote and the lowest vote wins everywhere: `SetTimeEverywhere(GetLowestTimeVote(...))`. With async time on, a vote applies only to the tickable it was cast for. A `PauseAll` command pauses every map and the world. [AsyncWorldTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncWorldTimeComp.cs)
- Pause-on-letter (a threat arriving) under async time pauses only the map concerned (or the world if there is no map context) and resets time votes; with async off it pauses everywhere. [AsyncTimePatches.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimePatches.cs)
- A newly generated hostile map is forced to normal speed for a short window. [AsyncTimePatches.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimePatches.cs)

**How maps at different times relate**
- A new map does not start at the world's time. Its clock starts at the latest clock among existing maps: `startingMapTicks = Find.Maps.Where(m => m != map).Select(m => m.AsyncTime()?.mapTicks).Max() ?? Find.TickManager.TicksGame;` and it starts paused. A multifaction option (`startingMapTimeFromBeginning`) starts a new faction's map at tick 0 instead. [MapSetup.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/Patches/MapSetup.cs)
- The date notifier (new day, season) is driven from the player home map "with the minimum timezone", using that map's `mapTicks` rather than the global count. [AsyncTimePatches.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimePatches.cs)
- In the time-system files read (AsyncTimeComp, AsyncWorldTimeComp, AsyncTimePatches, SetMapTime, MapSetup) there is no code that reconciles two maps' clocks, and none that handles caravans, trade, transport pods or other transfers between maps at different times. The storyteller and story watcher are global objects ticked from each map's tick. [AsyncTimeComp.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/AsyncTimeComp.cs); [SetMapTime.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/master/Source/Client/AsyncTime/SetMapTime.cs)

**Known problems**
- Issue #339 (opened 6 Nov 2022, still open): "World ticks can get ahead of map ticks/map ticks can get out of sync with each other (without async mode)". Cause as diagnosed by a maintainer on 15 Nov 2022: pause-on-letter fires while the map is holding a saved `TimeSnapshot`; when the map finishes its tick it restores the snapshot and un-pauses the tick manager, and the world reads the tick manager's current speed rather than its own desired speed. A tester reported that on a minor threat "the map pauses but the world does not", so map time falls behind world time. [rwmt/Multiplayer issue #339](https://github.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/issues/339)
- Other async-labelled issues: #76 pawns won't sleep in async mode (closed Aug 2021); #96 forming a caravan leads to a desync loop (closed Jul 2020); #419 child growth tier stays at zero with multiple factions and async time (closed Jul 2025); #539 exception when ticking quests during async time, collection modified during enumeration (opened Jun 2025, open); #571 async quest crash with multifaction (closed as duplicate Jul 2025); #964 cooldown issue in async time (opened Jul 2026, open); #969 async quest ticking error causing desync (closed Jul 2026). [rwmt/Multiplayer issues](https://github.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/issues?q=is%3Aissue+async+time)
- Player reports, forum posts only (Steam, Jan 2023): async time had been broken "since 1.4 adaptation"; "DO NOT use ASYNC time"; the fallback was manual etiquette, asking aloud "Need pause. Can we 3x?". The original poster wanted async so "one player [can] pause their colony while the other plays". [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/294100/discussions/0/3764479392870416354)
- A community note says quest sites with certain map features are messy under decoupled time and a map may need unpausing for a few seconds before they settle (search summary; page not fetched, exact page uncertain). [RimWorld Multiplayer hackmd](https://hackmd.io/@rimworldmultiplayer/BJBQwcPPlx)
- A secondary, auto-generated code wiki agrees with the above reading: each map keeps its own tick counter, speed and random state, synchronised through scheduled commands (search summary; fetch returned 429). [DeepWiki, async time system](https://deepwiki.com/rwmt/Multiplayer/7.1-async-time-system)

### Inferences
- This is "per-location clocks with no merge rule". It works in RimWorld because a colony map is nearly a closed system; the mod's own bug list is a catalogue of everything that is not closed: quests (global objects ticked from several clocks), cooldowns and ages (timestamps compared against whichever clock is current), caravans (entities crossing from one clock to another), pauses (a global flag written inside a per-map context).
- The bug in #339 is a general hazard of the snapshot-and-restore technique: any global written while a local context is swapped in is silently overwritten on restore. A design with per-location clocks should have no global "now" to swap; every time-dependent read should take the clock it means.
- Anything stamped with an absolute tick on one map and then moved to a map with a lower `mapTicks` is "from the future" there. The source read shows no translation of such timestamps on transfer. This is inferred from the absence of handling in the files read, not from an observed bug report, though #419 (growth) and #964 (cooldowns) are the kind of symptom it would produce.
- Starting a new map at the maximum of existing map clocks, paused, is a forward-only rule in miniature: a new location can never be created in another location's past.
- The world deriving its speed from the fastest unpaused map means the shared layer always keeps up with the most advanced participant, and slower maps are behind the world, never ahead of it (barring the pause bug). That is the same invariant KSP's mods reach by other means (section 5).
- With async off, lowest-vote-wins is the same unanimity rule as sleeping in survival games, and the Steam thread shows the same social cost: players negotiating speed aloud.
- Fit: the closest structural match (location-scoped clocks, host-ordered commands, deterministic per-location random streams, one interface for "world" and "place"). What it lacks is exactly what a split-and-reunite RPG needs: a rule for what happens when a character walks from a place at day 3 into a place at day 5, and one for shared objects (quests, factions) touched from both.

### Gaps
- The code that advances each tickable against real time (`TickPatch`) and the `ActualRateMultiplier` extension were not located, so whether a map can be forced to wait for, or catch up to, the world is unverified.
- Caravan, trade and transport-pod transfer code was not read; "no handling" is established only for the five time-system files listed.
- No design document or maintainer statement explaining the intended semantics of maps at different dates was found; the mod's settings text for async time could not be retrieved (404).
- The current (late 2026) reliability of async time is not established; the newest evidence is open issues from mid-2026.

## 5. Per-player timelines: Kerbal Space Program multiplayer mods (KMP, DarkMultiPlayer, Luna Multiplayer), with Dwarf Fortress, Kenshi, Starfield and No Man's Sky as lesser relatives

### Takeaway
The KSP mods give every player a private clock that can only run forward: warping puts you in your own "subspace" ahead of the others, you can still see and talk to everyone, you can only physically affect things in your own subspace, and the only way to rejoin someone is to jump forward to a subspace that is ahead of you. It is the cleanest existing precedent for per-player clocks with a forward-only sync, and its known costs are ghosts you cannot touch, objects popping in and out, unsolved cases where a place is "occupied in the future", and the loss of rewind (no quickload, no revert).

### Cited Findings
**Kerbal MultiPlayer (KMP, 2013-2014), the origin of the model (README fetched)**
- "KMP allows you to use regular 'on-rails' warp on-demand, as much as you need." Warping puts you ahead of other players and out of sync with them. [KMP README](https://github.com/TehGimp/KerbalMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md)
- Subspaces are the shared time states players occupy. You can sync with any player who is ahead of you via their Sync button, which moves you to their subspace; the main Sync button moves you to the latest subspace, which is also where new players join. [KMP README](https://github.com/TehGimp/KerbalMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md)
- "Be sure that you're landed or in a stable orbit when you sync, as otherwise you may find yourself somewhere unexpected!" [KMP README](https://github.com/TehGimp/KerbalMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md)
- You can still see, chat with and share craft designs with players in other subspaces. A player behind you is shown at a predicted position that "can change quickly while they keep playing"; a player ahead of you is in effect a replay of what has already happened. [KMP README](https://github.com/TehGimp/KerbalMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md)
- "Vessels from the past or future turn translucent so that you know that you won't be able to affect them." You cannot control vessels in a different subspace. [KMP README](https://github.com/TehGimp/KerbalMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md)

**DarkMultiPlayer (DMP)**
- The README describes the mod as supporting "subspace style (and master controlled warp) warping". [DMP README](https://github.com/godarklight/DarkMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md)
- Server warp modes (search summaries of the README forks and developer forum posts): `MCW_FORCE` (a player takes a warp lock and everyone follows; a subspace is created on exit); `MCW_VOTE` (the same, after a vote); `MCW_LOWEST` (everyone warps at the lowest requested rate; listed as not implemented); `SUBSPACE_SIMPLE` (not implemented); `SUBSPACE` (default: "each player can warp freely and sync to others' times", and the only mode that allows quicksave and quickload); `NONE` (no warp). Mode numbering differs between forks. [planteater8 fork README](https://github.com/planteater8/DarkMultiPlayer/blob/master/README.md); [azerothian fork](https://github.com/azerothian/darkmultiplayer); [KSP forum, DMP thread p.3](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/71656-darkmultiplayer-0380-ksp-1120/page/3); [p.6](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/71656-darkmultiplayer-0380-ksp-1120/page/6)
- The causality rule, from the developer's forum posts (search summary; pages returned 403 on fetch): "updates from the past are applied, but updates from the future are held back"; equivalently, things from the past affect the future instantly, but things from the future are held until your time passes the update. [KSP forum, DMP thread](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/71656-darkmultiplayer-0380-ksp-1120/page/16)
- The server does not keep history: its job is to hold enough data to put a player into the latest subspace; "It does not hold onto historic updates." (search summary). [KSP forum, DMP thread](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/71656-darkmultiplayer-0380-ksp-1120/page/19)
- Acknowledged paradoxes (developer posts, search summary): "you can literally drive through players that are in the future and cause them to explode"; and a player in the future can be sitting on the runway while you, in the past, are there before they launched. [KSP forum, DMP thread](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/71656-darkmultiplayer-0380-ksp-1120/page/47)
- On the alternative: a "warp master" sidesteps sync problems "at the cost of player boredom" (forum comment, search summary). [KSP forum, "Multiplayer time warp theory"](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/75753-multiplayer-time-warp-theory)

**Luna Multiplayer (LMP), DMP's successor (wiki fetched)**
- LMP "uses some ideas from DMP like the concept of subspaces." [LMP wiki, Home](https://github.com/LunaMultiplayer/LunaMultiplayer/wiki)
- Representation: on connect your clock is synced to server time. Warping creates your own subspace, stored on the server as a pair {subspace id, offset from server time}. Worked example from the wiki: server time Day 1 06:30:00; a 5-second warp at 2x puts you at 06:30:10; the server stores {1, 10} and sends it back. A player's time is server time plus their subspace's offset. [LMP wiki, Timewarp](https://github.com/LunaMultiplayer/LunaMultiplayer/wiki/Timewarp)
- What you see of others after warping ahead: a vessel in a stable orbit or on an escape trajectory is extrapolated to your time, as single player would do; a vessel that is suborbital or in atmosphere stays where it is, because the game "can't predict what other players will do, specially during atmospheric flight". [LMP wiki, Timewarp](https://github.com/LunaMultiplayer/LunaMultiplayer/wiki/Timewarp)
- What is given up: "Loading a savegame implies going back in time", so quickload is unavailable; "revert to flight" is implemented as removing your vessel from the server and respawning it, and "We cannot allow you to revert vessels from other players or when spectating"; "You can't pause an online game." [LMP wiki, Limitations](https://github.com/LunaMultiplayer/LunaMultiplayer/wiki/Limitations)
- Contracts whose state depends on shared, time-evolving objects (tourist and rescue contracts) are disabled because they are "quite difficult to synchronise between players". [LMP wiki, Limitations](https://github.com/LunaMultiplayer/LunaMultiplayer/wiki/Limitations)

**Player reception (KSP 2 multiplayer discussion threads, forum posts only, search summaries)**
- Description of the model by a supporter: all players "can be in their separate times and the game plays perfectly consistently, similar to single player, but you can also interact with other players' ships... you can only interact with ships that haven't been interacted with in the future"; to catch up "you just warp forwards until you are at the same time as them." [KSP forum, KSP 2 multiplayer thread p.58](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/187358-ksp-2-multiplayer-discussion-thread/page/58)
- Criticism: "the Luna/Dark implementation where players just pop in and out of existence in front of you and break the simulation by teleporting is not acceptable"; another poster objects to making players "work together" on when to sync or warp. [KSP forum, KSP 2 multiplayer thread p.62](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/187358-ksp-2-multiplayer-discussion-thread/page/62)
- A proposed refinement ("Leapfrog"): on docking, warp the lagging player only as far forward as needed to remove the spatial discrepancy, not all the way to the present. [KSP forum, "Time warp mechanic"](https://forum.kerbalspaceprogram.com/topic/203058-time-warp-mechanic)

**Dwarf Fortress (two time scales over one world, single player)**
- One tick is about 1 second (some pages say 0.5 s) in adventure mode and 72 seconds (1.2 minutes) in fortress mode, a ratio of 72:1; the faster fortress pace was chosen so players can watch a fort over years. [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Time](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Time)
- Since v0.40.01 ("world activation") the world keeps simulating after generation; when you start a new fortress or adventurer "the calendar is advanced by two weeks every time", and world activity otherwise runs at the current mode's speed. [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, World activities](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/DF2014:World_activities)
- Retiring a fortress leaves it as an active settlement in the world with citizens and most items intact. [Dwarf Fortress Wiki, Retire](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Retire)

**Kenshi (one clock, squads apart)**
- One global clock with player-chosen speed; only areas near player characters are loaded. Town squads are persistent and stored in the save when you leave; wilderness squads are "homeless" and deleted when they leave the loaded area (forum posts only). [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/233860/discussions/0/3276942988970004699); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/233860/discussions/0/1694922526900719892)
- Distant change is driven by "world states" (flags such as a leader's death) checked when an area loads, not by continuous simulation; mods extend the flags (mod pages). [Nexus Mods, Kenshi Kaizo](https://nexusmods.com/kenshi/mods/495)

**Starfield and No Man's Sky**
- Starfield keeps two clocks: universal time (UT) and each planet's local time. Waiting is denominated in local hours but advances UT by the planet's ratio: on Venus one local hour is 100 UT hours; on Titan, "if you wait for 24 hours Local Time... two weeks of Universal Time will pass". Players exploit Venus to pass UT quickly; a mod request asks for waiting in UT. [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/starfield/news/the-difference-between-ut-and-local-time-in-starfield); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/heres-why-venus-is-the-best-place-to-chill-out-in-starfield); [Nexus forums](https://forums.nexusmods.com/topic/13442633-ability-to-wait-in-universal-time)
- No Man's Sky at launch (2016): two players at the same spot did not see each other and "time of day and terrain damage varied"; time of day was individual to each player's own session. [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/two-no-mans-sky-players-just-reached-the-same-place-but-they-cant-see-each-other); [IBTimes](https://ibtimes.com/no-mans-sky-multiplayer-fans-say-ps4pc-games-missing-feature-proven-its-gameplay-2406124)

### Inferences
- The subspace model reduces to five rules that carry over to any medium: (1) each player has a clock equal to a shared base plus a personal offset; (2) offsets only grow; (3) to interact you must be in the same subspace, and the only way to get there is for the one behind to jump forward; (4) effects from the past are applied to the future at once, effects from the future are withheld until you reach them; (5) the server keeps the present state per object, not history, so nobody can go back.
- Rule 4 is what prevents contradictions, and it only holds if an object touched in the future becomes untouchable to those in the past (the supporter's "ships that haven't been interacted with in the future"; KMP's translucent vessels). The DMP developer's runway and drive-through examples are the residue: places and bystanders are not locked the way vessels are.
- LMP's extrapolation rule is a clean statement of a general principle: when the leader looks at something left behind, show it advanced only if its future is determined without its owner's choices (a stable orbit); otherwise show it frozen at its last known state.
- The costs are specific and named by players: the people around you are not really "there" (ghosts, pop-in), rejoining requires an explicit act, and the model forbids rewind. In text the visual costs largely vanish (no translucent ships to draw); the ghost problem becomes a narration problem (what do you say about a companion who is "still two days back"?).
- The jump forward has a physical cost in KSP (a ship mid-burn ends up "somewhere unexpected"). The equivalent for an RPG is that a character who jumps forward must be given an account of the skipped interval (rested, waited, travelled), and anything that would have interrupted them in that interval has to be resolved or ruled out. "Leapfrog" (advance only as far as needed) is the gentler variant.
- LMP disabling tourist and rescue contracts is the same lesson as RimWorld's quest bugs: shared objectives touched from more than one timeline are the hardest part, and the shipped answer was to remove them.
- DMP's menu of modes maps onto everything else in these notes: force (host skips for all), vote (Stardew, Solasta), lowest (RimWorld with async off), subspace (per-player clocks), none (NWN-style fixed clock).
- Dwarf Fortress and Starfield both show that a place or mode can have its own rate against a master calendar without paradox, as long as only one actor is advancing it at a time. Starfield also shows the exploit that follows when a wait is priced in local units: players go where the exchange rate is best.
- Kenshi's world-state flags evaluated on load are the cheap alternative to simulating distant places: record what was decided, apply consequences when someone next looks.
- Fit: strong for per-player clocks with forward-only sync, and better suited to text than to a 3D physics game. It needs additions the mods never built: rules for places and non-player characters (not only owned vessels) under rule 4, a narrated account of a forward jump, and a policy for shared quests.

### Gaps
- The DMP developer's forum posts could not be fetched (403); all DMP quotes come from search summaries and the page numbers attached to individual quotes are approximate. No dates were recovered for them.
- LMP's exact rule for which vessels a player may control or dock with across subspaces, and its lock system, were not read (the wiki's Lock system page was not fetched). Whether LMP offers DMP's other warp modes is unknown.
- No postmortem or developer talk on subspace design was found. No evidence was found on how groups actually behave under it (how often they sync, whether one player races ahead).
- Kenshi's off-screen behaviour rests on forum posts; no developer documentation was found. Current No Man's Sky time-of-day behaviour in co-op was not confirmed (sources are from 2016). Elite Dangerous was not researched. Mount and Blade co-op mods: only that Bannerlord Coop Campaign shares the campaign map and Warhost had clients wait in a lobby watching the host play the map ([ModDB, Bannerlord Coop](https://www.moddb.com/mods/bannerlord-coop); [ModDB, Warhost](https://www.moddb.com/mods/bannerlord-warhost)); nothing on their time control.

## 6. Contrast: hub-and-instance games (Sea of Thieves, Deep Rock Galactic, Monster Hunter)

### Takeaway
These games sidestep the problem: either the clock is a global wall-clock cosmetic that no player can move (Sea of Thieves), or time exists only inside a mission instance that the whole group enters and leaves together.

### Cited Findings
- Sea of Thieves: a full in-game day is 24 real minutes (one real minute per in-game hour), and "the time is always the same on every server, and switching to a different server won't speed things up or rewind the days" (search summary). [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/how-long-single-day-sea-thieves); [Sea of Thieves forum, "How Time is Measured"](https://www.seaofthieves.com/community/forums/post/852908)
- Sea of Thieves has no sleep, wait or fast-travel-with-time-cost action; the in-game pocket watch only reads the shared clock (search summary). [Sea of Thieves Wiki, Astronomy](https://seaofthieves.wiki.gg/wiki/Astronomy)

### Inferences
- A clock derived from real time and identical everywhere cannot produce contradictions and needs no consent, at the price of making time skipping impossible. It is the NWN persistent-world model without even a rest cooldown.
- The instance model's rule is "split up only between instances, never within one clock": the party is together whenever time matters. It is the opposite of the split-and-reunite requirement and is useful mainly as the baseline that the other models complicate.
- Fit: poor for a persistent sandbox with player-driven time, but the wall-clock model is a workable fallback mode for crowded shared spaces (a town everyone is in), where nobody should be able to move the clock.

### Gaps
- No sources were retrieved for Deep Rock Galactic or Monster Hunter; the statement that their time exists only inside missions is general knowledge, not cited here.
- The Sea of Thieves sources disagree on server-rotation intervals and on sunrise times; neither matters to the model.
