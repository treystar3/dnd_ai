# A3 exemplar pools: draft for the owner's approval

The pools for `data/rules/voice.yaml exemplars` (docs/design/09, A3). Each pool starts from the current exemplars, marked *(current)*,
plus 6 or 7 new lines in the same style:
- no names, places or figures;
- no dashes;
- none of the worn seed words (`voice.yaml worn.seed`) or stock phrases.

Approved by the owner on 2026-11-11 as a starting set, to be replaced by the game's own best passages from night runs as
the blind readers choose them.

How a pool is chosen: by what the engine knows at the call, never by the model.
- **arrival:** the turn the player reaches a place.
- **night:** dark hours.
- **interior:** an indoor place.
- **grief:** a death or loss in the moment.
- **fight:** combat narration.
- Otherwise the current general mix.
- Speech pools: **refusal** when the person declines, **gossip** when they pass on a rumour, **small talk** otherwise.

## Narration

### arrival
- *(current)* The gate stands open and nobody minds it. A dog looks up, decides against you, and goes back to sleep. Smoke from the chimneys leans east.
- The road gives out into mud and then into cobbles. A boy with a bucket stops to look at you, decides you are nobody, and goes on to the well.
- Washing hangs across the lane between two upper windows. Somewhere behind it a woman is arguing with a goat and losing.
- The bridge has been mended with planks of three different colours. On the far side a man is selling eels out of a barrel and not shouting about it.
- Bells from the chapel, then from somewhere further off, then nothing. The square fills with people who all seem to know where they are going.
- Two guards at the gate are sharing a pie. One of them lifts it an inch by way of greeting and does not stop chewing.
- The smell reaches you before the town does: tar, fish, bread, and under it the tannery, which nobody here notices any more.
- Hens in the road. A cart backing up with a lot of advice from the people watching. You wait, and so does everyone else.

### night
- *(current)* Night. The fire has gone to coals and somebody's horse is shifting in its sleep. You lie with a hand on the sword and listen to the river, which has nothing to say.
- The inn's last light goes out above you. After that there is only the dog two yards over, who has opinions about the moon.
- Rain starts sometime after midnight and finds the one gap in the roof. You move your boots and lie listening to it drip into them anyway.
- Owls. A cart that should not be on the road this late. Then the cart is gone and the owls are not.
- You wake once, sure someone said your name. The room is empty, the door is barred, and the fire has nothing more to tell you.
- Stars, more than you have seen in a month. The cold comes up out of the ground and settles in your hips.
- Down in the common room someone is still singing, the same verse over, each time a little further from the tune.

### interior
- Low beams, black with smoke. Every table has a cloth and every cloth has a burn in it.
- The shop is one room and most of it is barrels. The owner is behind them somewhere; you can hear him counting.
- Herbs hang from the rafters in bunches, labelled in a hand nobody else could read. It smells like a cupboard that has kept a secret for years.
- A long table, benches, a fire at the far end doing its best. The cat on the hearth has taken the warmest place and means to keep it.
- The counting house is cold on purpose. Ledgers on every shelf, and a clerk who does not look up until you have stood there long enough.
- Someone has swept the floor and stopped halfway. The broom is still leaning where they left it.
- The forge throws more heat than light. Tongs, hammers, a bucket gone black, and a horseshoe cooling on the anvil as if it is waiting to be asked.

### grief
- *(current)* He does not see you off. He has turned back to the nets before you reach the door, and the only farewell is the shuttle going on without you.
- She folds the shirt twice, puts it on the pile, and then takes it off the pile and holds it.
- Nobody has cleared his place at the table. They eat around it.
- The old woman thanks you for coming. She says it three times, as if the first two did not take.
- His tools are still on the bench in the order he left them. The boy will not let anyone move them.
- She talks about the price of salt, and her hands never stop working at the hem of her apron.
- There is a fresh mound behind the chapel with a jar of wildflowers on it. The flowers are a day old and somebody has already changed the water.

### fight
- *(current)* The axe comes down where your head was. You are already moving. Splinters sting your cheek, and then the thing is on you, all weight and stink, and the world narrows to its teeth.
- *(current)* The first one comes in low. You take it on the shield and the shock goes up your arm to the teeth. The second is already behind you.
- *(current)* It misses. The claw takes bark off the tree where your shoulder was and you are inside its reach now, close enough to smell it, and you put the knife in.
- *(current)* She goes down hard and does not get up. The one with the spear sees it and stops. Nobody moves. Then he runs.
- You are late on the parry and it costs you. The blade skids off your guard and opens your forearm, and the pain comes a breath later.
- It goes for the horse first. The horse has other ideas, and for a moment you have two enemies and one of them is on your side.
- The shield takes the blow and then the second, and the third splits it. You throw the pieces in its face and draw.
- Mud to the ankles. Every step is a bargain. The thing across from you is better at this ground than you are and it knows it.
- Your arrow takes it in the shoulder. It looks at the shaft as if offended, pulls it out, and comes on.
- You step in close where the spear is no use to him. He drops it and goes for his knife, and you are both very busy.

## Speech

### small talk
- *(current)* Horseshoes. Nails. If you want a sword you want the city, and more coin than you've got.
- *(current)* Keep walking, you. No, I mean it kindly. The gate shuts at dark and I'd sooner you were inside it than out.
- *(current)* If it isn't raining it's about to. Hand me that. No, the other one. Were you wanting something, or did you come in to drip?
- *(current)* Morning. You're the one with the wand. What can I do for you?
- *(current)* Thanks. Most don't bother saying it.
- Bread's gone up again. Don't ask me why. Ask the miller, he'll tell you it's the rain, it's always the rain.
- You're not from the valley. You walk like you expect the road to be there.
- Sit if you're sitting. The bench won't hold a man who leans.
- My sister's boy wants to go for a soldier. I told him, you'll see the world, and most of it will be the inside of a ditch.
- That dog's not mine. He just likes the step.
- If you're after the smith, he's at his mother's. If you're after his mother, good luck to you.

### refusal
- *(current)* You may ask. I may not answer. The dead keep their own counsel here, and so do I.
- *(current)* Later. I've a cart to get under cover before the hay's ruined. Ask me when it's done, or ask her.
- No. And don't stand there hoping I'll change it.
- I sold to somebody off the road once. Once.
- That's not mine to give, and if it were I still wouldn't.
- Come back with someone I know, and we'll talk.
- I've a family. You haven't asked me anything I can afford to answer.
- The door's behind you. It works the same going out.

### gossip
- *(current)* It took the ewe. Just took her. Didn't eat her, didn't drag her off, left her in the yard like a... like a message. You tell me what does that.
- You didn't hear it from me, but the reeve's been buying rope. A lot of rope.
- They say the widow up the hill has a man in the house. They say it like it's a bad thing.
- Three nights running there's been a light out on the marsh. Nobody's gone to look. Nobody's that stupid twice.
- The new priest drinks. Not much. Just enough that you'd notice, if you were me.
- The miller's boy and the smith's girl. Everyone knows. Everyone but the smith.
- Ask anyone and they'll tell you the bridge toll goes to the town. Ask who counts it, and they'll find something else to look at.
