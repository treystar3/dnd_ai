# fight_scene: The fight as the scene and the player meet it

This workstream closes the kind the audit calls "the fighter is a copy of the character": a pane command changes the character in the middle of a fight and the fighter, or its saved row, does not follow. It builds three pieces of infrastructure for that: one Character to Fighter step that can rebuild a fighter at any time and keeps what the fight itself did, with every field of a fighter declared as the character's or the fight's; the fight's row written with every operation's commit, so memory and row cannot differ when an operation ends; and one table of the `#` commands and of the standing questions (the second door into a fight) that says what each may do in a fight, with "locked" as the default. Beside that it mends two single places of the after-fight (the dead named in the wrong fight; captives who share one label and one note said three times) and places the one reading fault (a line in a fight that is no fight action costs the round) inside the reader's planned rework. It leaves to the owner: whether gear may change in a fight and what it costs; how captives are told apart and how often their four choices are said; who answers a look or a question in a fight, and whether an improvisation that names no effect is asked back before the fight's improvising is rebuilt; and every new line a player reads.
