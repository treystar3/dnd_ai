# task_failure: When a model task fails or a fault is swallowed

This workstream makes the game tell the truth when a model call fails, and makes the game's own faults leave a record instead of a made-up answer. It builds five pieces of infrastructure: what a step came to, kept on its operation and read by the road stop, the stand-ins and the step record; ledger rows that carry the facts their stand-in lists name, checked at startup; a telling asked as a passage of its own, with a ratchet that names every place still asking inline; the deferred queue taking a model failure under the age rule it already has; and a record-and-carry-on helper in engine/crashlog.py with a fence test, in two rings, that stops a new silent handler. The rest are small single-place mends, each with its own check. No feature adds a rule to a narrator or speaker prompt, a length limit or a sampling change. The owner is left the new and reworded stand-in lines (each marked "New lines: the owner reads them first") and the choices listed under "Owner decides". The audit scripts named under "Test first" lie in the audit's scratchpad folder audit/work/ (tf_harness.py in audit/work/task_failures/). Three features share their build with a neighbour and say so: TASK_FAILURE-16 with STALE_COPIES-10, TASK_FAILURE-8 with BUDGET_FLOW-8, TASK_FAILURE-3 with SITUATIONS-11. The review at the end says what was changed after the code was read again.

## Features

### TASK_FAILURE-1. What a step came to is kept on its operation, and a road stop pays only for help that worked
- Fixes: task-failures-3
- Pillar: 1 (A world that is real: prose and state agree) and 6 (Fun, deep, fair play); checklist 2, 4, 8
- Kind of case: any place that must know whether a step did what it set out to do, where today the code asks only "was no refusal row added" and so takes a waiting act, a failed roll or a failed ruling for a deed done.
- Build: engine/operation.py Operation gains `outcome` (done, partly, nothing, refused, waiting) and one setter `came_to(what)` beside `did()`; like `did` it is kept for the line and not saved. One reader gives a step's outcome: the field when it was set, else refused when the operation holds a `refused` row (the scan scenes/world.py run_step makes today moves into the reader, so a place that refuses by its own row is not missed), else done, as today. The engine sets the field where it settles the deed, after its own overrides: engine/act.py resolve from the degree it ends with (it turns a strong success into a success: success is done, partial is partly, a failure nothing), act.wait (waiting), act._end (a waiting act let go or lapsed: nothing), scenes/check_actions.py resolve after lucky_save, dark_charisma and patron_whispers have had their say, and scenes/law_actions.py where the theft knows whether it took. engine/checks.py roll does not set it: it returns the raw degree and its callers change it, one step can roll several checks (run_checks), and a failed roll is not always a deed that came to nothing (a dungeon hazard hurts and the move stands; a failed stealth roll opens the fight). So that no site is forgotten, the test reads the syntax tree for every call of checks.roll in game code (14 today: engine/act.py, combat_improv.py, matters.py twice, parley.py; scenes/arc_actions.py twice, camp_actions.py, check_actions.py, part_actions.py, pastime_actions.py, road_actions.py, standing_actions.py) and fails a caller that neither sets the outcome nor carries a comment tag saying its roll is not the deed's own outcome. scenes/road_actions.py stop_step reads the outcome of the step's operation in place of its `refused` argument: someone in need is paid (road_outcome, need_deed "road_help") only on done, and a hazard is marked dealt only on done, where today any improvised act marks it. A waiting act: act.wait is handed no intent, so stop_step, which has the intent and sees the outcome waiting, marks the character's newest pending row (store.load_act_pending) with the one in need when helps(intent, aid) holds; act.take_up, which rules the act at the next input, settles the help through the same reader, and only while that one is still the stop's aid (leaving_stop drops the aid, and a waiting act lapses when the character leaves the place). scenes/world.py run_step records `did` from the same reader. Everything takes the acting character's id, as act.py already does (stop_step reads self.player today). Not covered here: a check named on an improvised step skips the ruling and stop_step altogether; that is trace-turn-5, TYPED_LINE-8. The rule is the owner's: GOALS.md:212 (someone in need is helped only if the player helps).
- Test first: tools/sim/road_help_outcome_test.py, started from the audit script tf1_f3_roadstop.py. Failing today: at a stop with someone in need, an improvised act whose ruling call fails (the act only waits) is paid coin and reputation. Second cases: the same stop with a ruling of yes and a failed roll (no pay); a hazard with a waiting act about something else (not dealt); a waiting act ruled yes later by take_up (paid then, once); a waiting act whose stop is left before the ruling (never paid); a dungeon hazard whose roll fails and hurts (the move still reads as done); and the scan of checks.roll callers.
- Recordings: no. stub_short input 36 helps someone at a road stop and its ruling is a success ("Attempt: success", then the line that pays), so its pay stands and nothing moves; no other kept session acts at a stop. The outcome is not written to the record. Data hash: no.
- Measure: none (stub suite). Only branch (a) of the hazard question below changes what the ruling is asked; that branch is measured with tools/ruling_eval.py and needs the card.
- Size: M
- Needs: nothing
- Touches: stale_copies, travel_places, situations, typed_line, reload
- Owner decides: (1) Does a half success pay the helper's reward: no (recommended, by GOALS.md:212), or a share of it (a number in data). (2) What "the act dealt with the hazard" means: (a) the hazard is given to the ruling as the thing acted on, so the engine knows the act was about it (changes what the ruling is asked, so it is measured first), or (b) for now any act that worked at the stop deals with it, which already closes the waiting and failed cases (recommended).

### TASK_FAILURE-2. A fault the game carries on past is written down, and a fence stops a new silent handler
- Fixes: silent-excepts-1
- Pillar: infrastructure; checklist 4, 5, 7
- Kind of case: a broad `except Exception` in game code that passes, or answers for the fault, with no record anywhere.
- Build: engine/crashlog.py gains `carried(where, quiet=(), **context)`, a context manager: it lets core.interrupts.Interrupt through; an exception of a type named in `quiet` (a failure already filed elsewhere: an LLMError the gateway has logged, an OSError from writing a log) is counted and not written to crash.log, so a model failure is not called a crash and a replay that meets one is not reported as crashed; any other Exception is recorded with record() once per (where, error type) a session and only counted after that; then it carries on. Its once-store is a module dict capped at crashlog.KEEP (the number crash.log already keeps, so no new number), with a row in engine/owned.py so owned_state_633_test passes and the session puts it back. The fence is a new suite test that reads the syntax tree of the tracked game code (not tools/sim) in two rings, the two tables of docs/design/32_code_audit/tables/silent_excepts.md. Ring one is the audit's scan, a broad handler whose whole body is pass, continue, break or a return of a constant: 47 in game code, 20 sorted "leave" (the allowed list) and 27 still to mend. Ring two is the audit's wider set, a broad handler that only sets a default or writes a debug line: 39 counted by hand in game files and simbot.py, 16 of them sorted as hiding a fault or a state change with no record. A handler passes when it re-raises, calls crashlog, or stands in a list; the lists are keyed by file and function with a reason each and may only shrink. The same scan fails a broad handler round a gateway or embed call that does not re-raise Interrupt (engine/intent_audit.py:293 is one the tables lack; TASK_FAILURE-11 mends it). The helper's first users are the five ring-two handlers the table sorts as "record it once": engine/session.py:169 and :179 (a scene value that cannot be copied for the rollback is kept by reference), llm/embed.py:79 (the embedder turned off for the session; pinned by embed_test.py:56) and llm/runtime.py:181 and :185 (the server's context sized from a number in code when the measured ceilings cannot be read). The helper is for work no operation rolls back, or where a result already taken must not be lost; inside an operation the rule is to remove or narrow the guard (TASK_FAILURE-10). To say in the commit: record() grows crashlog.SESSION, so a replay that carries a fault is reported as crashed by tools/rerun.py; that is intended, and it is why a quiet type is kept out of record().
- Test first: tools/sim/carried_faults_test.py, started from the audit script silent_excepts_scan.py. Failing today: 27 handlers of ring one swallow a fault with no record. The test begins with them in a second list, "still to mend", each naming what empties it: TASK_FAILURE-10 takes 22, TASK_FAILURE-13 two, TASK_FAILURE-15 one, TASK_FAILURE-16 one, and scenes/dungeon_actions.py:191 is scenes1-13's, held by another workstream, so it stays on the list under that name until its feature lands. Second cases: a Cancelled raised inside `carried` comes out; the same ValueError raised sixty times at one place leaves one crash entry and a count; a quiet type leaves no crash entry and crashlog.SESSION as it was; the once-store never passes its cap.
