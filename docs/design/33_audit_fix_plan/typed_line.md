# typed_line: The typed line read by patterns

This workstream fixes what goes wrong between the line a player types and the step the engine runs: a repair that spends coin on a suspicion, an answer or a number read by its first matching word, a pattern over the whole line that overrules the step the plan already holds, a check that replaces the handler it should only colour, a hire who answers lines meant for someone else, and two extra model calls on four lines in ten. It builds six pieces of infrastructure: a count, on the tools that already replay played lines (tools/interp_corpus.py and tools/interp_eval.py, f365), of every repair, intercept and risk flag on lines people really typed; a mark in the action table for deeds that take coin or standing; one reader for an answer that names a choice; one reader for numbers said in words; a row in data for which check may stand in for which kind of step; and the design for the seam behind all of them (a handler is given its step, an intercept is an offer). Nothing here adds a repair or a word list for one misread (the owner's decision of 17 November, GOALS.md line 148): each pattern change makes an existing pattern do less, and each misread found becomes a case of the corpus in the words that were typed. It rides on docs/design/29_one_reader_plan.md (proposed, waiting on the owner) and adds only what the audit found. It leaves to the owner: whether the corpus may hold cases the game gets wrong today (owed), whether making an existing repair do less counts as a fix round, whether a suspected deed is asked first or left as the model read it, when the hire's follow-up becomes an offer, when the handlers' own reading is worked down, and the wording of any new line.
